L135: Bastar state was a 【59†princely state】 in India during the 【60†British Raj】. It was founded in the early 14th century by Annamaraja, a brother of the last ruler of the 【61†Kakatiya dynasty】, 【62†Prataparudra II】.
L136: 
L137: It is today used to refer to the same region, called 【63†Bastar division】 in 【64†Chhattisgarh】 state.^{【65†[1]】}
L138: In the early 19th century, the state became part of 【66†Central Provinces and Berar】 under the 【60†British Raj】, and acceded to the Union of India on 1 January 1948, to become part of 【67†Madhya Pradesh】 in 1956, and of the 【68†Bastar district】 of 【64†Chhattisgarh】 state in 2000. The current ceremonial ruler is Kamal Chandra Bhanj Deo, of the 【61†Kakatiya】 and 【69†Bhanj dynasty】.
L139: ## Overview
L140: 
L141: [【70†edit】]
L142: 
L143: Bastar state was situated in the south-eastern corner of the Central Provinces and Berar, bounded to the north by the 【71†Kanker State】, south by the Godavari district of 【72†Madras States Agency】, west by 【73†Chanda District】, 【74†Hyderabad State】, and the 【75†Godavari River】, and east by the 【76†Jeypore Estate】 in 【77†Odisha】.
L144: It had an area of 13,062 square miles (33,830 km^{2}) ^{【78†[2]】} and a population of 306,501 in 1901, when its capital city at 【54†Jagdalpur】, situated on the banks of 【79†Indravati River】, had a population of 4,762.^{【78†[2]】}
L145: ## History
L146: 
L147: [【80†edit】]
L148: 
L149: Traditionally the area is mentioned as 【81†Dandakaranya】 in the epic 【82†Ramayana】, and part of the 【83†Kosala Kingdom】 in the 【84†Mahabharata】. Around 450 AD, the Bastar area was ruled by a 【85†Nala】 king called Bhavadatta Varman, who is recorded as having invaded the neighbouring 【86†Vakataka】 kingdom during the reign of its king, 【87†Narendrasena】 (440–460)^{【88†[3]】}
L150: A brother of 【62†Prataparudra II】, Annamaraja, has been associated with ruling what eventually became the state of Bastar. This appears likely to be historical revisionism, dating from a genealogy published by the ruling family in 1703, because the document records only eight generations spanning almost four centuries of rule. Such revisionism and tenuous claims of connection to the Kakatiyas was not uncommon because it was perceived as legitimising the right to rule and a warrior status.
L151: Talbot notes that there is a record of a brother called Annamadeva and that:
L152: > He is said to have left Warangal for the northeast after anointing Prataparudra's son as king. Thus, the founder of the family fortunes in Bastar may very well have been a Telugu warrior from Telangana who was familiar with the prevalent legends about the Kakatiyas.^{【89†[4]】}
L153: According to this chronology, the state was established around 1324 CE and the founder established his kingdom at Bastar under the tutelage of a local goddess, Danteshwari. That goddess remains the tutelary deity of Bastar region and the 【90†Danteshwari Temple】 stands today at 【91†Dantewada】.^{【92†[5]】} He ruled till 1369 when he was followed successively by Hamir Deva (r.
L154: 1369–1410), Bhaitai Deva (1410–1468), Purushottama Deva (1468–1534), Pratapa Raja Deva (1602–1625) and Dikpala Deva (1680–1709), after which the Bastar branch of the dynasty became extinct in the third generation with him, after which a descendant of the younger brother of Prataparaja Deva, Rajapala Deva became the next King in 1709.
L155: Rajapala Deva had two wives, first a Baghela princess, married, who had a son, Dakhin Singh, secondly, a 【93†Chandela】 Princess, who has two sons, Dalapati Deva and Pratap, trouble however struck again when after the death of Rajapala Deva in 1721, the elder queen ousted other claimants and placed her brother on the throne of Bastar, Dalapati Deva took refuge in the neighbouring 【94†kingdom of Jeypore】 and finally regained his throne a decade later in 1731.^{【95†[6]】}^{【96†[7]】}
L156: Its capital was Jagdalpur, where Bastar royal palace built by its ruler, when its capital was shifted here from old capital Bastar.^{【95†[6]】}
L157: 
L158: Later at some point in the 15th century Bastar was divided into two kingdoms, one based in Kanker and the other ruled from Bastar.^{【97†[8]】} The present Halba Tribe claims to descend from the military class of these kingdoms.
L159: Until the rise of the 【98†Marathas】, the state remained fairly independent until the 18th century. In 1861, Bastar became part of the newly formed Central Provinces and Berar, and in 1863, after years of feud, over the 【99†Kotapad】 region, it was given over to the neighbouring 【94†Jeypore state】 in 1863, on the condition of payment of tribute of Rs. 3,000, two-thirds of which sum was remitted from the amount payable by Bastar.
L160: By virtue of this arrangement the tribute of Bastar was, reduced to a nominal amount.
L161: 【100†Pravir Chandra Bhanj Deo】 (1929–1966), the 20th and the last ruling head of the Bastar state, ascended the throne in 1936, before it acceded to India in 1948 during the 【101†political integration of India】.^{【102†[9]】}
L162: Pravir was immensely popular among the tribals. He was shot dead in a "police action" on 25 March 1966 while leading a tribal movement against encroachment of land by outsider in concert with the authorities in Bastar. He was executed on the steps of his own Palace in Jagdalpur. Scores of other tribals and courtiers too were murdered by the police.
