L141: Gaekwads (also spelled as Gaikwads, Gaekwars) (【75†IAST】: Gāyakavāḍa), was a Hindu 【76†Maratha】 dynasty of the former 【77†Maratha Empire】 founded by Chatrapati Shivaji Raje Bhonsle. Its subsequent (erstwhile) 【78†princely state】 of 【66†Baroda】 in 【79†western India】 from the early 18th century until 1947.^{【80†[1]】}^{【81†[2]】} The ruling prince was known as the 【82†Maharaja】 Gaekwad of Baroda.
L142: With the city of 【83†Baroda】 (【84†Vadodara】) as its capital, during the 【85†British Raj】 its relations with the British were managed by the 【86†Baroda Residency】. It was one of the largest and wealthiest princely states of 【87†British India】, with its wealth coming from the lucrative cotton business as well as rice, wheat and sugar production.^{【88†[3]】}
L143: 【89†Laxmi Vilas Palace】 of the Gaekwad dynasty.
L144: ## Early history
L145: 
L146: [【90†edit】]
L147: 
L148: A print of Maharaja Sayajirao Gaikwad
L149: 
L150: The Gaekwad rule of 【84†Baroda】 began when the 【91†Maratha】 general 【68†Pilaji Rao Gaekwad】 conquered the city from the 【92†Mughal Empire】 in 1721. The Gaekwads were granted the city as a 【93†Jagir】 by 【94†Chhatrapati】 【95†Shahu I】, the emperor of the 【77†Maratha Empire】 and 【96†Peshwa Bajirao I】 played an important role in helping 【68†Pilaji Rao Gaekwad】 strengthen his position in 【83†Baroda】 and establish his rule there.^{【97†[4]】}
L151: In their early years, the Gaekwads served as subordinates of the 【98†Dabhade】 family, who were the Maratha chiefs of Gujarat and holders of the senapati (commander-in-chief) title. When 【99†Umabai Dabhade】 joined 【100†Tarabai】's side against 【101†Balaji Baji Rao】, Pilaji's son 【102†Damaji Rao Gaekwad】 commanded the Dabhade force. He was defeated, and remained in Peshwa's custody from May 1751 to March 1752. In 1752, he was released after agreeing to abandon the Dabhades and accept the Peshwa's suzerainty.
L152: In return, Damaji was made the Maratha chief of Gujarat, and the Peshwa helped him expel the Mughals from Gujarat.^{【103†[5]】}
L153: Damaji subsequently fought alongside 【104†Sadashiv Rao】, 【105†Vishwas Rao】, 【106†Malhar Rao Holkar】, 【107†Jankoji Scindia】, Sidhoji Gharge-Desai (Deshmukh) and 【108†Mahadji Shinde】 in the 【109†Third Battle of Panipat】 (1761). After the Maratha defeat at Panipat, the central rule of the Peshwas was weakened.
L154: As a result, the Gaekwads, along with several other powerful Maratha clans, established themselves as virtually independent rulers, while recognizing the nominal authority of the Peshwas and suzerainty of the 【110†Bhonsle】 Maharaja of 【111†Satara】.
L155: ## British suzerainty
L156: 
L157: [【112†edit】]
L158: 
L159: Sayajirao with Richard Temple, the Governor of Bombay and other members of the court. Circa 1880
L160: 
L161: The Gaekwads, together with several 【113†Maratha】 chieftains, fought the 【114†British】 in the 【115†First Anglo-Maratha War】.
L162: On 15 March 1802, the British intervened to defend the Gaekwad Maharaja, 【116†Anand Rao Gaekwad】, who had recently inherited the throne against rival claimants, and the Gaekwads concluded the Treaty of Cambey with the British that recognized their independence from the 【117†Maratha Confederacy】 and guaranteed the 【82†Maharajas】 of Baroda local autonomy in return for recognizing British suzerainty, though it was a result of an inability to overpower them.
L163: 【118†Maharaja Sayaji Rao III】, who took the throne in 1875, did much to modernize Baroda, establishing compulsory primary education, a library system and the 【119†Maharaja Sayajirao University of Baroda】. He also encouraged the setting up of 【120†textile】 factories, which helped create Baroda's textile industry. He is known for offering 【121†B. R. Ambedkar】 a scholarship to study at 【122†Columbia University】.
L164: Upon 【123†India】 attaining its independence in 1947, the last ruling Maharaja of 【84†Baroda】, Pratapsinhrao, acceded to India. Baroda was eventually merged with 【124†Bombay State】, which was later divided, based on linguistic principle, into the 【125†states】 of 【126†Gujarat】 and 【67†Maharashtra】 in 1960.
L165: 
L166: Gaekwad or Gayakwad also survives as a fairly common Maratha 【127†surname】, found mainly in the Indian state of 【67†Maharashtra】.
