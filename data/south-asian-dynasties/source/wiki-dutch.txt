L137: Not to be confused with 【68†Dutch East Indies】.
L138: 
L139: A View of Chinsura the Dutch Settlement in Bengal (1787).
L140: Dutch India (【69†Dutch】: Nederlands Voor-Indië) consisted of the settlements and trading posts of the 【70†Dutch East India Company】 on the 【71†Indian subcontinent】. It is only used as a geographical definition, as there was never a political authority ruling all Dutch India. Instead, Dutch India was divided into the governorates 【72†Dutch Ceylon】 and 【73†Dutch Coromandel】, the commandment 【74†Dutch Malabar】, and the directorates 【75†Dutch Bengal】 and 【76†Dutch Suratte】.
L141: The Dutch Indies, on the other hand, were the 【68†Dutch East Indies】 (present-day 【77†Indonesia】) and the 【78†Dutch West Indies】 (present-day 【79†Suriname】 and the former 【80†Netherlands Antilles】).
L142: ## History
L143: 
L144: [【81†edit】]
L145: Dutch presence on the 【71†Indian subcontinent】 lasted from 1605 to 1825.
L146: Merchants of the 【70†Dutch East India Company】 first established themselves in 【73†Dutch Coromandel】, notably 【82†Pulicat】, as they were looking for textiles to exchange with the spices they traded in the East Indies.^{【83†[1]】} 【76†Dutch Suratte】 and 【75†Dutch Bengal】 were established in 1616 and 1627 respectively.^{【84†[2]】}^{【85†[3]】} After the Dutch conquered 【86†Ceylon】 from the Portuguese in 1656, they took the Portuguese forts on the Malabar coast five years later as well, as both were major spice producers, to create a Dutch monopoly for the spice trade.^{【87†[4]】}^{【88†[5]】}
L147: Apart from textiles, the items traded in Dutch India include 【89†precious stones】, 【90†indigo】, and 【91†silk】 across the Indian Peninsula, 【92†saltpeter】 and 【93†opium】 in Dutch Bengal, and 【94†pepper】 in Dutch Malabar. Indian slaves were exported to the 【95†Spice Islands】 and the 【96†Cape Colony】.
L148: In the second half of the eighteenth century, the Dutch lost their influence more and more following the 【97†Travancore–Dutch War】. The 【98†Kew Letters】 relinquished all Dutch colonies to the British, to prevent them from being overrun by the French. Although Dutch Coromandel and Dutch Bengal were restored to Dutch rule by the 【99†Anglo-Dutch Treaty of 1814】, they returned to British rule owing to the provisions of the 【100†Anglo-Dutch Treaty of 1824】.
L149: Under the terms of the treaty, all transfers of property and establishments were to take place on 1 March 1825. By the middle of 1825, therefore, the Dutch had lost their last trading posts in India.

L151: 
L152: [【101†edit】]
L153: 
L154: Gold 【102†pagoda】 with an image of Lord 【103†Venkateswara】, a form of the Hindu god 【104†Vishnu】, issued at the Dutch mint at 【82†Pulicat】, c. 17th or 18th century.
L155: 
L156: Dutch mints in 【105†Cochin】, 【106†Masulipatnam】, 【107†Nagapatnam】, 【108†Pondicherry】 (for the five years 1693–98 when the Dutch had gained control from the French), and 【82†Pulicat】 issued coins modeled on local Indian coinages.^{【109†[6]】} Coins struck included:
L157:   * the puttan (single and double)^{【109†[6]】}^{【110†[7]】}
L158:   * the fanam^{【111†[8]】}
