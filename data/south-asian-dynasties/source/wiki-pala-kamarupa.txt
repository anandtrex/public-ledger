L142: The Pala dynasty of 【45†Kamarupa kingdom】 ruled from the 10th century CE. Like the 【44†Pala Empire】 of 【60†Bengal】, the first ruler in this dynasty was elected, which probably explains the name of this dynasty "Pala".^{【61†[2]】} The Hindu orthodoxy drew their lineage from the earlier 【62†Varman dynasty】 and thus ultimately from 【63†Narakasura】 i.e. 【64†Bhauma dynasty】. The dynasty is unrelated to the previous Varman and 【54†Mlecchna】 dynasties.^{【65†[3]】}
L143: The Palas were the last dynasty to rule Kamarupa. After the collapse of the Pala rule, Kamarupa disintegrated, to be followed in due course by the 【66†Ahom】,^{【67†[4]】} 【68†Chutia】,^{【69†[5]】} 【58†Kamata】,^{【70†[6]】} and 【55†Kachari】^{【71†[7]】} kingdoms, and the confederate rule of the 【57†Baro-Bhuyans】.^{【72†[8]】}
L144: ## History
L145: 
L146: [【73†edit】]
L147: The term "Kamarupa" rarely appeared in the records of the Pala rulers; instead they used 【74†Pragjyotisha】, the legendary kingdom of the epics, to 【75†legitimise】 their authority.^{【76†[9]】} The Pala kings of Kamarupa assumed the title of paramadaivata paramabhattāraka mahārājādirāja (the imperial title of the 【77†Guptas】), sri-vārāha (the one who can trace his origin to 【78†Varāha】) and prāigjyotisādhipati (the ruler of Prāgjyotisa).^{【79†[10]】} The Pala dynasty came to an end when Kamarupa was invaded by the 【80†Gaur】 king 【81†Ramapala】 (c.
L148: 1072–1126).^{【82†[11]】} Timgyadeva was made the governor of Kamarupa who ruled between 1110 and 1126.^{【83†[12]】} Timgyadeva threw off the yoke of the Pala king and ruled independently for some years when he was attacked and replaced by Vaidyadeva under Ramapala's son 【84†Kumarapala】. Vaidyadeva, who ruled between 1126 and 1140^{【85†[13]】}, declared independence within four years of his rule after the death of Kumarapala.
L149: Both Timgyadeva and Vaidyadeva issued grants in the style of the Kamarupa kings (three copper plates attached to the seal of the Kamarupa kings by a ring). The work of the Pala dynasty of Kamarupa is reflected in the 【86†Madan Kamdev】 sculpture.
