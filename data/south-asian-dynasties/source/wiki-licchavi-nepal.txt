L152: The Licchavis of Nepal (【107†Sanskrit】: लिच्छवि, also Lichchhavi, Lichavi) ruled over a kingdom in the 【108†Kathmandu Valley】 of 【106†Nepal】 from approximately 400 to 879 CE.
L153: The Licchavi clan originated from a branch of the 【63†Licchavis of Vaishali】, who ruled in the territory of modern-day 【109†Bihar】 and who later conquered the Kathmandu Valley.^{【110†[4]】}^{【111†[5]】}^{【112†[6]】} The Licchavis were ruled by a maharaja, aided by a prime minister and other royal officials, but in practice local communities were controlled by 【113†caste councils】.^{【114†[7]】}
L154: The ruling period of this dynasty was called the Golden Period of Nepal. A table of the evolution of certain Gupta characters used in Licchavi inscriptions prepared by Gautamavajra Vajrācārya can be found online.^{【115†[8]】}
L155: ## Records
L156: 
L157: [【116†edit】]
L158: 
L159: It is believed that a branch of the 【117†Licchavi】 clan, having lost their political fortune and military power in 【118†Vaishali】 (【109†Bihar】),^{【119†[9]】} came to 【120†Kathmandu】 and intermarried with the family of the ruling Queen, Mandeva Shree Vogini of the 【121†Nagvanshi】 clan, thus beginning their rule in Nepal.^{【122†[10]】} They also battled with local militias in 【123†Chyasal】 to gain control of Nepal.
L160: The earliest known physical record of the kingdom is an inscription of 【124†Mānadeva】, which dates from 464. It mentions three preceding rulers, suggesting that the Licchavi dynasty began in the late 4th century.
L161: ## Government
L162: 
L163: [【125†edit】]
L164: 
L165: The Licchavis were ruled by a 【126†Maharaja】 ("great king"), who was aided by a prime minister, in charge of the military and of other ministers. Nobles, known as 【127†samanta】 influenced the court whilst simultaneously managing their own landholdings and militia. At one point, between approximately 605 and 641, a prime minister called 【64†Amshuverma】 assumed the throne.
L166: The population provided land taxes and conscript labour (vishti) to support the government. Most local administration was performed by village heads or leading families. Many kings ruled but the popular ones were 【128†Manadeva】, 【64†Amshuverma】 etc.
L167: ## Culture
L168: 
L169: [【129†edit】]
L170: ### Language
L171: 
L172: [【130†edit】]
L173: 
L174: During the Licchavi dynasty, 【99†Sanskrit】 was the court language, while 【131†Newar language】 was the vernacular language.^{【132†[11]】} The Licchavi inscriptions are 190 in total, and are carved in Sanskrit. Among those inscriptions, 53 were written in 【133†Gupta script】, while 137 were written in 【134†Kutila script】.^{【135†[12]】} Although these inscriptions were written in Sanskrit, they contain frequent Newari derivative words.^{【132†[11]】}^{【136†[13]】}
L175: ## Economy
L176: 
L177: [【137†edit】]
L178: 
L179: The economy was agricultural, relying on 【138†rice】 and other grains as staples. Villages (grama) were grouped into dranga for administration. Lands were owned by the royal family and nobles. Trade was also very important, with many trading settlements.

L181: ## Geography
L182: 
L183: [【139†edit】]
L184: ### Domain
L185: 
L186: [【140†edit】]
L187: 
L188: Settlements already filled the entire valley during the Licchavi period. Further settlement extended east toward 【141†Banepa】, west toward 【142†Tistung Deurali】, and northwest toward present-day 【143†Gorkha】.
