L127: Coinage of 【50†Rajadhara Manikya】 (1586–1599 CE), king of Tripura.
L128: The Manikya dynasty was the ruling house of the 【43†Twipra Kingdom】 and later the princely 【44†Tripura State】, what is now the 【51†Indian】 state of 【52†Tripura】. Ruling since the early 15th century, the dynasty at its height controlled a large swathe of the north-east of the 【53†Indian subcontinent】.
L129: After coming under 【54†British】 influence, in 1809 they transitioned from feudal monarchs into rulers of a 【55†princely state】, though the Manikyas maintained control of the region until 1949, when it ascended in union with India.
L130: ## History
L131: 
L132: [【56†edit】]
L133: The 【57†Rajmala】, the chronicle of the kings of Tripura, traces the Manikyas descent from Druhya, a son of the legendary 【42†Lunar dynasty】 monarch 【58†Yayati】.^{【59†[1]】}^{【60†[2]】} An unbroken line of 144 (likely mythological) Tripura monarchs is subsequently listed up to the ascension of one 【61†Ratna Fa】, who is stated to have become the first Manikya after being granted the cognomen by the 【62†Sultan of Bengal】.^{【63†[3]】} However, it is now believed that the Rajmala had been mistaken in the genealogy and chronology of the initial Manikya rulers.^{【64†[4]】} Numismatic evidence suggests that the first historical Manikya was in fact 【45†Maha Manikya】,^{【65†[5]】} a 【66†Tripuri】 chief who founded 【43†the kingdom】 after establishing dominance over neighbouring tribes in the early 1400s.^{【67†[6]】} This monarch then took the title "Manikya" in honour of a historic victory over Bengal, with the name being inherited by his descendants.^{【68†[7]】}
L134: Maha Manikya's early successors achieved considerable military success, conquering territory in Bengal, 【69†Assam】 and 【70†Burma】. Tripura reached its zenith in the 16th century under such prominent kings as 【71†Dhanya Manikya】 and 【72†Vijaya Manikya II】,^{【73†[8]】} with its lands stretching from the 【74†Garo Hills】 in the north to the 【75†Bay of Bengal】 in the south.
L135: As monarchs of a Hindu kingdom, the Manikyas developed a rivalry with the successive Muslim rulers of Bengal, coming into conflict with 【76†Sultans】, governors and 【77†Nawabs】 before being brought under 【78†Mughal】 suzerainty in the early 17th century. As Mughal power waned, the antagonism with Bengal re-erupted, which drove the Manikyas to first approach the 【54†British】 for aid.
L136: In 1761, Tripura succumbed to British influence, becoming 【44†a princely protectorate】, though control of the region remained under the Manikya dynasty.^{【79†[9]】}
L137: In 1870, 【80†Bir Chandra Manikya】 ascended the throne and began a series of political reforms to his kingdom, modelling his government on the British system. A lover of the 【81†culture of Bengal】, 【82†Bengali】 was adopted by the court under his rule and he developed a friendship with the poet, 【83†Rabindranath Tagore】.
L138: After Tripura was briefly incorporated into the province of 【84†Eastern Bengal and Assam】 at the beginning of the 20th century, the last Manikya monarch, Maharaja 【85†Bir Bikram Kishore】, chose to come under the jurisdiction of the predominantly Hindu 【86†Dominion of India】 in 1947.
L139: The 【87†final ascension】 of Tripura into the modern 【51†Indian】 nation was signed by his widow, 【88†Kanchan Prava Devi】, in place of the minor 【47†Kirit Bikram Kishore】, bringing to an end five centuries of Manikya rule.^{【79†[9]】}
