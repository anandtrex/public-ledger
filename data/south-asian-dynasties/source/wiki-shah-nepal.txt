L179: The Shah dynasty (【111†Nepali】: शाह वंश, romanized: Shāh Vaṃśa), also known as the Shahs of Gorkha or the Royal House of Gorkha, was a Nepalese 【87†Thakuri】 dynasty that ruled the 【112†Gorkha Kingdom】, one of the 【113†Chaubise】 principalities, from 1559 to 1768. Following the 【114†unification of Nepal】 under 【115†Prithvi Narayan Shah】, the dynasty ruled the 【88†Kingdom of Nepal】 from 25 September 1768 until the 【116†monarchy】 was abolished on 28 May 2008.^{【117†[2]】}
L180: The Shah kings claimed descent from 【118†Rajput】 chiefs who migrated to the Himalayan hills during the 【119†medieval period】. According to the genealogical chronicles (lit.
L181: (literal translation) 'vamsavalis') of Nepal, the Shahs were descendants of the rulers of 【120†Chittaur】 who fled from 【121†Central India】 during the 【122†Muslim invasions of the twelfth and thirteenth centuries】.^{【123†[3]】} 【124†Dravya Shah】, the youngest son of Yasho Brahma Shah of 【125†Lamjung】, seized control of the small hill state of Gorkha in 1559, establishing the dynasty's rule over the 【126†principality】.^{【127†[4]】} Over the following two centuries, the Shah rulers consolidated their position in the Gorkha region, and by the mid-eighteenth century, Gorkha had emerged as one of the most ambitious state of the Chaubisi principalities.
L182: The dynasty's most transformative period began with the accession of 【115†Prithvi Narayan Shah】 to the Gorkha throne in 1743.
L183: He launched a campaign to unify the fragmented hill states of present-day Nepal, beginning with the annexation of the Chaubisi principalities and culminating in the conquest of the 【128†Kathmandu Valley】 in 1768.^{【123†[3]】} Rather than annexing the newly acquired territories to Gorkha, Prithvi Narayan Shah moved his capital to Kathmandu, establishing the Shah dynasty as the ruling house of a unified Nepal.
L184: His successors expanded the kingdom's borders, but the Shah monarchs' power was later seized by the hereditary 【129†Rana】 prime ministers, who dominated Nepal from 1846 to 1951.^{【123†[3]】} The dynasty continued as a 【130†constitutional monarchy】 until 2008, when the 【131†Constituent Assembly】 voted to abolish the monarchy, ending 240 years of Shah rule.^{【132†[5]】} The last Shah king, 【90†Gyanendra】, was given fifteen days to vacate the royal palace, which was subsequently converted into a museum.^{【132†[5]】}
L185: ## History
L186: 
L187: [【133†edit】]
L188: 
L189: The Shah dynasty traces its historical ancestry to King of 【134†Kaski】, 【135†Kulamandan Khand Shah】, whose grandson 【124†Dravya Shah】 conquered the 【136†Liglig】 from local 【137†Magar】 King Dalsur Ghale Magar^{【138†[6]】} with the help of accomplices from six resident clans of Majhkot and Liglig. Dravya Shah named his new kingdom 【112†Gorkha】.
L190: The origins of the Shah dynasty are deeply rooted in the historical landscape of 【139†Magarat】 and Tamuwan, an ancient confederation in western Nepal inhabited primarily by the 【137†Magars】 in the Western and Central hills (【140†Palpa】, 【141†Tanahu】, 【142†Gulmi】, Arghakhanchi, Syangja, 【143†Myagdi】, Parbat, 【144†Baglung】, 【145†Pyuthan】, 【146†Rukum】, 【147†Rolpa】, Kushma, Musikot, Ghiring, Rising, 【136†Liglig】, and Gorkha, which were historically part of the Magarat region as it was known as the Magar homeland before the unification of Nepal), and 【148†Gurung】 in the Central hills (Lamjung, Gorkha, Kaski, Tanahu).
L191: Before the Shah came to power, 12 Magarat—located in the Western and Central hills—was ruled by various Magar kings, including the powerful Magar kings of Palpa.^{【149†[7]】}^{【150†[8]】}^{【151†[9]】} Meanwhile, 18 Magarat, located west of the Kali 【152†Gandaki river】 and primarily inhabited by the 【153†Kham Magar】, was governed by Magar rulers,^{【154†[10]】}^{【155†[11]】} alongside Malla-Khas influence in some areas, and Tamuwan was ruled by a Native Gurung tribal chief.
L192: After the decline of 【139†Magarat】's unity, the region fragmented into two groups of small kingdoms: the 【156†Baise Rajya】 (22 principalities) in the far west and the 【113†Chaubisi Rajya】 (24 principalities) in central Nepal. These kingdoms were mostly ruled by local 【87†Thakuri】 and 【157†Magar】 chieftains. Among the 【113†Chaubisi Rajya】 states was 【112†Gorkha】, where the Shah dynasty rose to power by replacing a local chief.
L193: In 1559 CE, Dravya Shah, the son of a Lamjung ruler, seized control of 【112†Gorkha】 by defeating the Magar king 【158†Mansingh Khadka Magar】 with the support of local elites. The Shah dynasty combined their Khas-Thakuri heritage with the martial traditions of 【139†Magarat】, gradually expanding their influence by conquering neighboring principalities.
L194: This process of unification culminated in 1768 CE under 【115†Prithvi Narayan Shah】, who established the modern Kingdom of Nepal, marking the Shah dynasty as both inheritors and transformants of 【139†Magarat】's legacy.
L195: ## Coronation of Dravya Shah
L196: 
L197: [【159†edit】]
L198: 【124†Dravya Shah】 was the youngest son of 【160†Yasho Brahma Shah】, 【161†Raja】 (king) of Lamjung and grandson of 【89†Kulamandan Shah Khad】, Raja of Kaski.^{【162†[12]】} He became the king of 【112†Gorkha】 with the help of his accomplices: Gangaram Rana Magar, Narayan Das Arjyal, Kaji Ganesh Pandey. He ascended the throne of Gorkha in 1559 A.D. The loose translation of the Nepali work known as the "Wright Chronicle"^{【163†[13]】} describes the coronation of Dravya Shah thus:
L199: > On Wednesday the 8th of 【164†Bhadon】 Badi, 【165†Saka】 1481 (A.D. 1559) 【166†Rohini Nakshatra】 (i.e. the moon in the Rohini mansion) being an auspicious day, Drabya Shah aided by Bhagirath Panth, 【167†Ganesa Pande】, Gangaram Rana Magar, Narayan Das Arjyal, Khanal Bohra and Murli Khawas of Gorkha, concealed himself in a hut.
L200: Ganesa Pande had collected all the people of who wore the brahmanical thread such as the 【168†Thapas】, Busals, Ranas and Maski Ranas of the 【137†Magar】 tribe, they went by the Dahya Gauda route and the Durbar. Drabya Shah killed the King 【158†Mansingh Khadka Magar】^{【162†[12]】} his own hand, with a sword, during the battle ensued. At the same auspicious moment Drabya took his seat on the 【169†gaddi】, amidst the clash music.

L202: ## Absolute monarchy (1768–1846)
L203: 
L204: [【170†edit】]
L205: 
L206: King 【115†Prithvi Narayan Shah】, the last king of 【112†Gorkha Kingdom】 (1743–1768) and the first Shah king of 【171†Nepal】 (1768–1775)
L207: In 1743, 【115†Prithvi Narayan Shah】 became the ruler of Gorkha. He declared war on other principalities, defeating them one by one. On 25 September 1768, he established the 【114†unified kingdom of Gorkha】. He became the first king of the unified kingdom named as 【172†Asal Hindustan】. He, his sons and their successors continued fighting and defeating other kingdoms and enlarging the kingdom of Gorkha. In 1814, the 【173†Anglo–Nepalese War】 between 【174†Gorkha】 and the 【175†East India Company】 began.
L208: By 1815, the Shah king had been thoroughly defeated. By 1816, Gorkha had lost one-third of its territory. The Shah kings continued to rule as absolute monarchs until 1846, when the political order changed from 【176†absolute monarchy】 to 【130†constitutional monarchy】.
L209: ## Hereditary prime ministers (1846–1951)
L210: 
L211: [【177†edit】]
L212: In 1846, the 【129†Rana dynasty】 gained power in Nepal. The Ranas became prime ministers and reduced the King's status to a figurehead position. The Ranas ruled Nepal as hereditary prime ministers though in the name of the figurehead king. In 1950, the Shah king 【178†King Tribhuvan】 went into exile in India. He and his family, including the crown prince 【179†Mahendra】, later returned.
L213: After India became a 【180†secular state】 in 1950, and the remaining rajas retired, Nepal was the only remaining 【181†Hindu】 kingdom. In 1951, with the help of India, a popular politician common man 【182†Matrika Prasad Koirala】 became the prime minister of Nepal. Tribhuvan returned to Kathmandu. The Shah dynasty regained control and the prime minister, 【183†Mohan Shamsher Jang Bahadur Rana】, resigned. 【178†King Tribhuvan】 ruled until 1955 and King 【179†Mahendra】 ruled until 1972.
L214: Mahendra's son, 【184†Birendra】, became king.
L215: ## Constitutional monarchy (1990–2008)
L216: 
L217: [【185†edit】]
L218: 
L219: King 【186†Birendra Bir Bikram Shah】; the first constitutional monarch of Nepal
L220: 
L221: In 1990, under King 【184†Birendra】, Nepal became a 【130†constitutional monarchy】 after a mass movement from people forced Birendra Shah to restore democracy.^{【187†[14]】}
L222: ### Massacre of the royal family
L223: 
L224: [【188†edit】]
L225: On 1 June 2001, some members of the Shah dynasty were 【189†murdered in the royal palace】. A High Commission inquiry report concluded that the royal family was slaughtered by Crown Prince 【190†Dipendra】. This remains controversial.^{【191†[15]】} Among the dead were the Crown Prince's father, King Birendra and his brother, 【192†Prince Nirajan】. After the attack, Dipendra was in a coma and was declared king for a short time. He died a few days later.
L226: 【90†Gyanendra Bir Bikram Shah Dev】, Dipendra's uncle, took the throne. In February 2005, he dismissed the parliament in order to govern in his own right.
L227: ## Abolition of the Shah monarchy
L228: 
L229: [【193†edit】]
L230: 
L231: 【90†King Gyanendra Bir Bikram Shah】; The Last Monarch of Nepal. After a decade of 【194†Civil War】, monarchy was abolished. (2001-2008)
L232: After Jana Andolan II, the King Gyanendra restore the assembly which was dissolved in 1999 ad, and following it, the parliament unilaterally strip power enshrined in 1990 constitution, and by it, it marked de facto end of 1990 constitution(which was formed after Jana Andolan I).^{【195†[16]】} On 24 December 2007, 【196†Nepal's Provisional Parliament】 met.
L233: It was decided that the monarchy would be abolished in 2008 after the 【197†Constituent Assembly elections】.^{【198†[17]】} The motion enjoyed overwhelming support in the chamber, passing by a 270-vote majority. Of the 329 sitting members of parliament, only three voted against abolishing the monarchy.^{【199†[18]】} It was decided that for the time being, Gyanendra would retain his title and continue residing in the Royal Palace, albeit stripped of all political power and authority.^{【199†[18]】}
L234: On 28 May 2008, following scheduled elections, the 【131†1st Nepalese Constituent Assembly】 declared Nepal a 【200†Federal Democratic Republic】 and the monarchy was abolished, removing the Shah dynasty from power. 【201†Kul Bahadur Gurung】 said of the 601 member assembly, 560 voted in favour, 4 were against and 37 were absent or abstained.
L235: Following an Assembly agreement involving the 【202†Nepali Congress】 and both Nepalese Communist parties, (the 【203†Leninists】 and the much larger 【204†Maoist faction】), Gyanendra stepped down.
L236: Gyanendra vacated the palace in 【99†Kathmandu】 which later became a museum. Until they could find permanent accommodation, the royal couple were offered residence as commoners at the Nagarjuna Palace, a former royal summer residence. The Nagarjuna palace lies in forested hills about eight kilometres (five miles) northwest of Kathmandu.
L237: ## Post-Abolition Loyalism
L238: 
L239: [【205†edit】]
L240: The dynasty was conclusively deposed in 2008 due to the 【189†Nepalese royal massacre】 and the subsequent 【194†Nepalese Civil War】. However, there are still loyalists to the dynasty, as evidenced by the 【206†Rastriya Prajatantra Party】, the 【207†2023 Nepalese pro-monarchy protests】 and 【208†2025 Nepalese pro-monarchy protests】.
L241: During the 【209†2025 Nepalese Gen Z protests】, a group of protesters also expressed loyalty to the abolished monarchy and rallied in support of its restoration.^{【210†[19]】}^{【211†[20]】}
