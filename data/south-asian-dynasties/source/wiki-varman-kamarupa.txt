L143: The Varman dynasty (350–650) was the first historical dynasty of the 【71†Kamarupa kingdom】.
L144: It was established by 【64†Pushyavarman】, a contemporary of 【72†Samudragupta】.^{【73†[2]】}^{【74†[3]】} The earlier Varmans were subordinates of the 【75†Gupta Empire】,^{【76†[4]】} but as the power of the Guptas waned, 【77†Mahendravarman】 (470–494) performed two 【78†horse sacrifices】 and the status of Kamarupa as an independent state remained unimpaired.^{【79†[5]】} As per the Apsad Inscription of Adityasen, Susthivarman was defeated by Mahasengupta on the bank of Lauhitya.^{【80†[6]】} The first of the three Kamarupa dynasties, the Varmans were followed by the 【70†Mlechchha】 and then the 【81†Pala】 dynasties.^{【82†[7]】}
L145: ## Origin
L146: 
L147: [【83†edit】]
L148: 
L149: The name "Kāmarūpa" in later 【84†Brahmi script】, in the 【85†Allahabad Pillar inscription】 of 【72†Samudragupta】 (350-375 CE).^{【86†[8]】}
L150: The first king in this dynasty was 【64†Pushyavarman】, possibly a contemporary of 【72†Samudragupta】 (c.
L151: 335/350-375 CE).^{【74†[3]】} The kingdom which he established with much effort, grew in the periphery of the 【75†Gupta Empire】, adopted the north Indian political model, and its kings took on names and titles of the Gupta kings and queens.^{【87†[9]】} Nothing much is known directly about the initial kings till the sixth king, Mahendravarman, who established a rock temple and assumed the title of Maharajadhiraja (king-of-kings) in the last quarter of the fifth century.^{【87†[9]】} The dynastic line from Pushyavarman first appear in the 7th century, in 【88†Dubi】 and 【89†Nidhanpur】 copperplate inscriptions issued by Bhaskaravarman and in the 【90†Harshacharita】 and not earlier in any inscription from his ancestors.^{【82†[7]】}^{【87†[9]】} In these inscriptions 【66†Bhaskaravarman】 claims that he was a descendant of 【91†Narakasur】, 【92†Bhagadatta】 and 【93†Vajradatta】.^{【82†[7]】} Modern scholars consider this claim to be a fabrication, jae-eun shin suggests that this genealogy was created in the context of Bharkarvarman's alliance with 【94†Harshavardhana】, to help 【95†legitimise】 his sovereignty.
L152: The use of Naraka/Bhagadatta lineage to establish sovereignty continued under the 【70†Mlechchha】 and the 【81†Pala】 dynasties, a practice which is in keeping with the trend in the post-Gupta period in India.^{【87†[9]】}
L153: She-Kia-Fang-Che claiming him to be a kshatriya whose ancestors came from China.^{【96†[10]】} Though some modern scholars have opined that the Varman dynasty is probably of Indo-Aryan descent,^{【97†[11]】}^{【98†[12]】} it is now believed that the Varmans were originally non-Indo-Aryans. 【99†Suniti Kumar Chatterjee】 calls Bhaskaravarman a Hinduised Mlechcha king of Indo-【100†Mongoloid】 origin.^{【101†[13]】} Hugh B. Urban also infers that the Varmans descended from non-Aryan tribes.^{【102†[14]】}

L155: ## Capital
L156: 
L157: [【103†edit】]
L158: 
L159: The capital was moved at least once, the last time by 【104†Sthitavarman】 (566-590) with the older city not named but presumed to be 【57†Pragjyotishpura】,^{【105†[15]】} located at the south-eastern slope of the Narakasur hill near 【106†Dispur】. The new capital was possibly some location in 【107†Guwahati】.^{【108†[16]】} Though the claim is not supported by any archaeological findings.
