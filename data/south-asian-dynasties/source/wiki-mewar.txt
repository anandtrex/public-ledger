L159: Mewar, also spelled as Mewad, is a historical region located in the south-eastern part of the 【93†Indian state】 of 【81†Rajasthan】.
L160: It includes the present-day districts of 【84†Udaipur】, 【85†Rajsamand】, 【86†Bhilwara】, 【87†Chittorgarh】 and 【88†Pratapgarh】 in Rajasthan.^{【94†[1]】} The language of this region is known as 【91†Mewari】, one of the 【95†dialects】 of the 【96†Rajasthani language】 spoken as a 【97†lingua franca】 by the different ethnic groups assembled altogether in one identity called the Mewaris.^{【98†[2]】}
L161: ## Background
L162: 
L163: [【99†edit】]
L164: 
L165: For centuries, the region was ruled by 【100†Rajputs】 as 【71†Kingdom of Mewar】. During the period of 【101†British East India Company】, it became a 【102†princely state】 as 【103†Udaipur】. It emerged as an administrative unit during the period governance in India and remained until the end of the 【104†British Raj】 era.
L166: The Mewar region lies between the 【105†Aravali Range】 to the northwest, 【106†Ajmer】 to the north, Gujarat and the 【107†Vagad】 region of Rajasthan to the south, the 【108†Malwa】 region of Madhya Pradesh state to the south and the 【109†Hadoti】 region of Rajasthan to the east.
L167: ## Etymology
L168: 
L169: [【110†edit】]
L170: The word "Mewar" is vernacular form of "Medapata" (【111†IAST】: Medapāṭa), the ancient name of the region. The earliest epigraph that mentions the word "Medapata" is a 996–997 CE (1053 【112†VS】) inscription discovered at Hathundi (【113†Bijapur】). The word "pata" or "pataka" refers to an administrative unit. According to the historian G. C.
L171: Raychaudhuri, Medapata was named after the Meda tribe, which has been mentioned in 【114†Varāhamihira】's Brihat-Samhita.^{【115†[3]】} The 1460 【116†Kumbhalgarh】 inscription associates the Medas with Vardhana-giri (modern 【117†Badnor】 in Mewar region).^{【118†[4]】} Historian Sashi Bhusan Chaudhuri associates the ancient Medas with the modern 【119†Mer people】.^{【120†[5]】}
L172: The 1285 CE (1342 VS) 【121†Mount Abu】 (【122†Achaleshwar】) inscription of the 【123†Guhila】 king 【124†Samarasimha】 provides the following etymology while describing the military conquests of his ancestor 【125†Bappa Rawal】 (Bappaka): and the [MEWAR] name was given by 'lord' [SHIVA] to the [Bappaka] then this dynesty got new name that is known as [ MEWAR] till now .
L173: "This country which was, in battle, totally submerged in the dripping fat ('medas' in 【126†Sanskrit】) of wicked people by Bappaka bears the name of Śrī Medapāṭa." Historian Anil Chandra Banerjee dismisses this as a "poetic fancy", but acknowledges the 'terrible' battles fought between the Rajputs and the Arabs.^{【127†[6]】}
L174: ## Geography
L175: 
L176: [【128†edit】]
L177: The northern and eastern portions of Mewar are made up of an elevated plateau while the western and southern portions were rocky and hilly with dense forests.^{【129†[7]】} The watershed divide between drainage of the 【130†Bay of Bengal】 and drainage of the 【131†Gulf of Khambhat】 runs almost through the centre of Mewar.^{【132†[8]】} The northern and eastern part of Mewar is a gently sloping plain, drained by the Bedach and 【133†Banas River】 and its tributaries, which empty northwest into the 【134†Chambal River】, a tributary of the 【135†Yamuna River】.
L178: The southern and western part of the region is hilly, and marks the divide between the Banas and its tributaries and the headwaters of the 【136†Sabarmati】 and 【137†Mahi】 rivers and their tributaries, which drain south into the 【131†Gulf of Khambhat】 through 【138†Gujarat】 state. The 【139†Aravalli Range】, which forms the northwestern boundary of the region, is composed mostly of sedimentary rocks, like 【140†marble】 and 【141†Kota Stone】, which has traditionally been an important construction material.
L179: The region is part of the 【142†Khathiar-Gir dry deciduous forests】' 【143†ecoregion】. Protected areas include the 【144†Jaisamand Wildlife Sanctuary】, the 【145†Kumbhalgarh Wildlife Sanctuary】, the 【146†Bassi Wildlife Sanctuary】, the 【147†Gandhi Sagar Sanctuary】 and the 【148†Sita Mata Wildlife Sanctuary】.
L180: Mewar has a 【149†tropical】 climate. Rainfall averages 660 mm/year, and is generally higher in the southwest and lower in the northeast of the region. Over 90% of the rain typically falls in the period of June to September every year, during the 【150†southwest monsoon】.
L181: 
L182: According to the 2011 Census of India this region has a population of 9,045,726 people.
