L146: The Gauḍa kingdom was a kingdom during the 【76†Classical era】 in the 【77†Indian subcontinent】, which originated in the 【55†Gauda】 region of 【56†Bengal】 (modern-day 【78†West Bengal】 and 【75†Bangladesh】)^{【79†[3]】}^{【80†[4]】} in 4th century CE or possibly earlier.^{【81†[5]】}
L147: ## Location and extent
L148: 
L149: [【82†edit】]
L150: 
L151: A Buddhist 【83†Mahāyāna】 Text 【84†Mañjuśrī-Mūlakalpa】 records the existence of Gauda kingdom in Bengal before it was replaced by 【85†Gupta Empire】 in the 4th century. King Loka who was born in Vardhamāna (【86†Bardhamān】) is mentioned who must have ruled in the early 4th century CE.^{【87†[6]】}
L152: King 【59†Shashanka】 is often attributed to expand 【55†Gauḍa】's influence over 【56†Bengal】 which unified the region under one crown.
L153: He reigned in 7th century, and some historians place his rule approximately between 593 and 636.^{【88†[7]】}^{【89†[8]】}^{【90†[9]】} His capital was at 【91†Karnasubarna】, 9.6 kilometres (6.0 mi) south-west of 【92†Baharampur】, headquarters of Murshidabad district.^{【79†[3]】} The Chinese monk, 【93†Xuanzang】 (Hiuen Tsang) travelled from the country of Karnasubarna to a region in the present-day state of Orissa ruled by Shashanka.^{【80†[4]】} There is mention of 【94†Pundravardhana】 being part of Gauda in certain ancient records.^{【95†[10]】} Not much is known about the early life of Shashanka.
L154: Historian D K Ganguly is reported to have concluded that he was a native of Magadha.^{【96†[11]】} The same source reports that the historian 【97†Padmanath Bhattacharya】 took Shashanka to be a son of Mahāsenagupta. R D Banerji concluded that he was descended from the Magadha Guptas. 【98†Nagendranath Basu】 has argued that Shashanka was the son / descendant of Raja Karnadeva, who founded the city of 【91†Karnasubarna】 in Bengal.^{【99†[12]】}

L217: 
L218: In some sources, Shashanka is described as a tribal leader of Bengal.^{【127†[14]】} Shashanka's name appears in multiple forms, including Śaśānka and Śaśānka-deva. The name is derived from 【128†Sanskrit】, as another name for the Moon. Śaśānka-deva therefore loosely translates to "moon god".
L219: The Hindu god 【129†Shiva】 bears the epithet Shashankasekhara, meaning, "he who bears the moon". The Chinese monk 【93†Xuanzang】's writings, he is mentioned as She-Shang-Kia..^{【130†[15]】} In Sinha's Dynastic History of Magadha, the names 'Śaśānka' and 'Soma' are used interchangeably.
L220: Some historians believe that Shashanka began his career as a feudatory chief (maha 【131†samanta】) under Mahasenagupta, of the 【70†Later Gupta Dynasty】.^{【132†[16]】} And that after the death of Mahasenagupta, Shashanka drove the later Guptas and other prominent nobles out of the region and established his own kingdom with his capital at 【91†Karnasubarna】.
L221: Other historians like Sailendra Nath Sen is of the opinion that Mahasenagupta - already under pressure from the Maukharis (for failing to provide adequate protection) - wouldn't have knowingly appointed Shashanka to such an important position. Middleton (2015) argues in a similar vein that Shashanka served as maha samanta to a Gauda king, possibly 【67†Jayanaga】.^{【133†[17]】} Whether Shashanka was a feudatory under the Maukharis or the Guptas is not known.
L222: But he ruled from Karnasuvarna.^{【134†[18]】} According to Bana, he was the king of Gauda^{【135†[19]】} and was addressed as Maharajadhiraja (king of great kings).^{【135†[19]】}Following his death, Shashanka was succeeded by his son, 【68†Manava】, who ruled the kingdom for eight months. However Gauda was soon divided amongst 【136†Harshavardhana】 and Bhaskarvarmana of 【73†Kamarupa】, the latter even managing to conquer Karnasuvarna.
L223: Evidence is inconsistent regarding links of Gauda with the 【137†Rarh region】. While Krishna Mishra (11th or 12th century), in his Prabodha-chandrodaya, mentions that Gauda Rashtra includes Rarh (or Rarhpuri) and Bhurishreshthika, identified with 【138†Bhurshut】, in 【139†Hooghly】 and 【140†Howrah】 districts, the Managoli inscription of the 【141†Yadava】 king 【142†Jaitugi I】 distinguishes Lala (Rarh) from Gaula (Gauda).^{【79†[3]】}
L224: According to Jain writers of the thirteenth and fourteenth centuries, Gauda included Lakshmanavati in present-day 【143†Malda district】.^{【79†[3]】}
L225: The 【144†Pala emperors】 were referred to as Vangapati (Lord of Vanga) and Gaudesvara (Lord of Gauda). 【145†Sena】 kings also called themselves Gaudesvara. From then Gauda and Vanga seem to be interchangeable names for the whole of Bengal.^{【79†[3]】} Even, in the early 12th-century Sanskrit text 【146†Manasollasa】 (1129 CE), the name Gauda-Vangala was mentioned to denote the whole 【147†Bengal region】.^{【148†[20]】}
