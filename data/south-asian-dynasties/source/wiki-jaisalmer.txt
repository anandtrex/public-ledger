L166: The Kingdom of Jaisalmer was a kingdom in the far-western part of present-day 【106†Rajasthan】, India, from the mid-12th century CE until 1947. In 1156 CE, 【107†Rawal Jaisal】 moved his capital from 【108†Lodhruva】 to 【109†Jaisalmer】 because the former was vulnerable to attacks from Turko-Afghan and Baloch tribes.
L167: The descendants of Jaisal continued to exercise absolute control over Jaisalmer until 1818 CE, when a treaty of 【110†subsidiary alliance】 with the 【111†British East India Company】 bringing under British protection and sphere of influence. Known as the Maharawal, the native ruler of the princely state was entitled to a 15-gun salute.^{【112†[2]】}
L168: ## History
L169: 
L170: One Rupee coin of 1756, from the Princely state of Jaisalmer, minted during the reign of Ranjit Singh.The Bhati kingdom, marked as Multan in 800 CE
L171: The Bhatis of 【109†Jaisalmer】 belonged to the 【113†Bhati】 dynasty, a 【114†Yaduvanshi】 clan of 【115†Rajputs】.^{【116†[3]】}^{【117†[4]】} They reportedly originated in 【118†Mathura】 through a common ancestor named 【119†Rao Bhati】, who is claimed to be a descendant of 【120†Pradyumna】.^{【121†[5]】}^{【122†[6]】} According to the seventeenth-century 【123†Nainsi ri Khyat】, the Bhatis after losing Mathura moved to 【124†Bhatner】 in 【125†Lakhi Jungle】, and from there to other locations in 【126†western】 and 【127†northwestern India】 including 【106†Rajasthan】 and 【128†Punjab】.
L172: Rao Bhati conquered and annexed territories from 14 princes in Punjab, including the area of what is now modern-day 【129†Lahore】.^{【130†[7]】}^{【131†[8]】} He is also credited with establishing the modern town of 【132†Bathinda】 in the Lakhi Jungle area in the 3rd century.^{【121†[5]】}According to 【133†Satish Chandra】, the 【134†Hindu Shahis】 of Afghanistan made an alliance with the Bhati rulers of 【135†Multan】, because they wanted to end the slave raids made by the Turkic ruler of 【136†Ghazni】, however the alliance was defeated by 【137†Alp-Tegin】 in 977 CE.^{【138†[9]】}
L173: In 1156, 【107†Rawal Jaisal】 moved his capital from 【108†Lodhruva】 to 【109†Jaisalmer】 and the state took its name from this new capital.
L174: 
L175: 【107†Rawal Jaisal】, founder of 【139†Jaisalmer Fort】. 【139†Jaisalmer Fort】, built in 1156 AD by the 【115†Rajput】 Rawal (ruler) 【107†Jaisal】.
L176: Around 1299 CE, Rawal Jait Singh I faced a long siege by 【140†Alauddin Khalji】 of 【141†Delhi Sultanate】, who is said to have been provoked by a Bhati raid on his treasure caravan. By the end of the siege, facing certain defeat, the Bhati Rajput women committed '【142†Jauhar】', and the male warriors under the command of Mularaja met their fatal end in battle with the Sultan's forces.
L177: For a few years after the successful siege, the fort remained under the sway of 【141†Delhi Sultanate】, before being eventually reoccupied by some surviving Bhatis.^{【143†[10]】}
L178: In 1541 CE, Rawal Lunakaran also fought 【144†Mughal】 emperor 【145†Humayun】 when the latter attacked the fort on his way to 【146†Ajmer】.^{【147†[11]】} He also offered his daughter in marriage to emperor 【148†Akbar】. Mughals controlled the fort until 1762.^{【149†[12]】}
L179: The treaty between the 【150†East India Company】 and Mulraj on 12 December 1818 allowed the Mulraj to retain control of the fort and provided for protection from invasion. After the death of Mulraj in 1820, his grandson Gaj Singh inherited control of the fort.^{【149†[12]】}
L180: ### Modern history (Princely state of Jaisalmer)
L181: 
L182: On 11 December 1818 Jaisalmer became a 【151†British protectorate】 in the 【152†Rajputana Agency】.^{【153†[13]】}^{【154†[14]】}
L183: 
L184: Map of Jaisalmer State with the 【155†duchies】 of Satto, Pithala, Loharki, Lakhmana, Didhu, Kanod, Tota, Bhadariya, and Nachna
L185: Traditionally, in the Middle Ages, the main source of income for the kingdom was levies on 【156†caravans】, but the economy was heavily affected when 【157†Bombay】 emerged as a major port and sea trade replaced the traditional land routes. Maharawal Ranjit Singh and Bairi Sal Singh attempted to turn around the economic decline but the dramatic reduction in trade impoverished the kingdom.
L186: A severe 【158†drought】 and the resulting 【159†famine】 from 1895 to 1900, during the reign of Maharawal Salivahan Singh, only made matters worse by causing widespread loss of the livestock that the increasingly agriculturally based kingdom relied upon.
L187: The attempts of Maharawal Jawahir Singh (1914–1949) at modernization were also not entirely successful in turning the kingdom's economy around, and the drylands of Jaisalmer remained backward compared with other regions of 【99†Rajputana】, especially the neighbouring state of 【160†Jodhpur】.
L188: Nonetheless, the extensive water storage and supply, sanitation, and health infrastructures developed in the 1930s by the prime minister Dewan Bahadur Brijmohan Nath Zutshi provided significant relief during the severe droughts of 1941 and 1951. Maharawal During 1930–1947, Jawahir Singh and his ministers also promoted technical education and the academic disciplines of civil and mechanical engineering in the state.
L189: After the departure of the British from India in 1947, the Maharawal signed an 【161†Instrument of Accession】 to the new 【162†Union of India】, while retaining some internal autonomy until the 1950s.
