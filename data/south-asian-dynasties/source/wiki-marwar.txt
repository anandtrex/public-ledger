L201: Kingdom of Marwar, also known as 【103†Jodhpur】 State during the modern era, was a kingdom in the 【89†Marwar】 region from 1243 to 1818 and a 【98†princely state】 under British rule from 1818 to 1947. It was established in Pali by Rao Siha, possibly a migrant 【120†Gahadavala】 noble, in 1243. His successors continued to struggle against regional powers for domination and 9 out of 15 rulers till 1438 died in combat.
L202: In 1395, its capital was changed to 【102†Mandore】 by Rao 【121†Chunda of Mandore】 and to Jodhpur in 1459 by 【122†Rao Jodha】. The kingdom was established and ruled by 【123†Rathore】 clan of 【124†Rajputs】.
L203: Marwar struggled and resisted against the 【109†Mughals】 under the rule of 【125†Rao Ganga】 and 【90†Maldeo Rathore】 who is known to be one of the greatest warriors of the time. The kingdom remained independent until it was annexed by the 【109†Mughal Empire】 in 1581 after the death of 【126†Chandrasen Rathore】. It remained under direct 【109†Mughal】 control until 【127†Udai Singh】 was restored to the 【128†throne】 as a vassal and given the title of Raja in 1583.
L204: During the late 17th century it was under the strict control of the Mughal Emperor 【129†Aurangzeb】, but the ruling 【130†house of Rathore】 was allowed to remain semi-autonomous in their territory.
L205: Marwar came into a long period of dispute and war with the Mughals when 【131†Raja Jaswant Singh】 died in 1678 and was supposed to be succeeded by his posthumous-born son 【132†Ajit Singh】 but Ajit was not appointed the ruler by Aurangzeb. During this time 【133†Durgadas Rathore】 struggled to preserve the 【123†Rathore dynasty】 and freed Marwar from the Mughal Empire after 31 years of war.
L206: The 【134†British】 had no role in the state's affairs until 6 January 1818, when the Raja at that time, 【135†Man Singh】, entered into a 【136†subsidiary alliance】, after which the Rajas of Marwar (or Jodhpur) continued as rulers of a princely state.
L207: During the 【137†Indian Rebellion of 1857】, the Rajput Nobles under Thakur 【138†Kushal Singh of Auwa】 led a rebellion against Maharaja 【139†Takht Singh】 and the British, however the rebellion was put to an end by the British armies under Colonel Holmes after a siege of the Thakur's fort in 【140†Auwa】.^{【141†[4]】} The armies of Jodhpur State fought in World War I for the British. They actively fought in Afghanistan and the Middle-east and scored a series of victories for the British empire.
L208: The Jodhpur lancers with the support of the Mysore lancers defeated a large host of Turks and Germans in the 【142†Battle of Haifa (1918)】. Some of the other battles they participated in were the battles of 【143†Suez】, 【144†Gaza】, 【145†Jordan Valley】, 【146†Abu Tellul】 and 【147†Megiddo】.
L209: Following 【148†Indian independence】 in 1947 【108†Maharaja Hanwant Singh】, the last ruler of Jodhpur state signed the Instrument of Accession on 11 August 1947 and merged his state in the Union of India.
L210: ## Geography
L211: 
L212: [【149†edit】]
L213: Covering an area of 93,424 km^{2} (36,071 sq mi), Jodhpur State was the largest state under the 【117†Rajputana Agency】 and the third largest state in 【134†British India】, after 【150†Jammu and Kashmir State】 and 【151†Hyderabad State】.
L214: The state's revenue was 5 crores during its peak; however, it halved due to decline by the time of Maharaja Bijai Singh.^{【152†[5]】} The average revenue of the state during the British era was Rs.56,00,000 in 1901.^{【153†[6]】} The Maharaja's of Jodhpur originally had a gun salute of 19 guns with 21 personal. However the gun salute was reduced to 17 guns and 19 personal in 1870, due to a quarrel with the Maharana of Udaipur.^{【154†[7]】}^{【155†[8]】}

L307: Location of Marwar within 【195†Rajputana】 circa 1525, with neighbouring Politics.^{【196†[9]】}^{【197†[10]】}
L308: Recent genealogists believe that the Rathores are connected to the 【120†Gahadavala dynasty of Kannauj】.
L309: A connection is often established between Rajput ruler 【198†Jaichand】 and the Rathores.^{【199†[11]】} Jaichand was later defeated by 【200†Muhammad of Ghor】 in the 【201†Battle of Chandawar】.^{【202†[12]】} The one branch of the defeated rulers of Kannauj migrated westwards into Rajputana and were invited to settle in 【101†Pali】.^{【203†[13]】} 【204†Siha】 is regarded as the first emigrant in the region to establish his kingdom in Pali in 1243 when he helped the locals drive away the Meds and 【205†Meenas】 and assumed the title of Rao.^{【206†[14]】}^{【207†[15]】} He died fighting an invading 【208†Muslim】 force in 1273.
L310: In this invasion large amounts of Brahmins in the region were massacred.^{【209†[16]】}
L311: 【204†Siha's】 son 【210†Rao Asthana】 captured Khed from the 【114†Guhilas】 and Idar from the 【211†Bhils】.^{【212†[17]】}^{【213†[18]】} He died fighting a force of 【214†Jalaluddin Khilji】 in 1291 and was succeeded by his son Dhuhar.^{【215†[19]】}^{【216†[20]】} Dhuhar captured Mandore from the Pariharas but lost it later.^{【217†[21]】}
L312: Dhuhar's son Raipal (r.1309-1313) and grandson Karanpal followed by Karanpal's son Bhim who continued to fight the Kingdom of Malwa and the Turkic invaders.^{【218†[22]】} Karanpal's son Jhalansi defeated the Sodha Rajputs, and the ruler of Multan but died fighting the Turks in 1328.^{【219†[23]】} His son Chhada defeated the Sodha's, the ruler of Jaisalmer, Nagaur and Jalore.^{【220†[24]】} He died fighting the Songaras in 1344.^{【221†[25]】} Chhada's son Rao Tida avenged his father's death by defeating the Songara's.^{【222†[26]】} Tida was succeeded by his son Tribhuvan and his grandson Mallinath.
L313: Mallinath fought with the Songaras, Deoras, Bhatis and the Tughluqs.^{【223†[27]】}
L314: ### Rao Chunda
L315: 
L316: [【224†edit】]
L317: In 1384, Mallinath's nephew 【121†Chunda】 gained the title of Rao and became the ruler.^{【225†[28]】} Chunda married in a Parihar Rajput clan from which he received the city of Mandore as Dowry. He shifted his capital to that city in 1395.^{【226†[29]】} In 1396, Zaffar Khan of Gujarat laid siege to Mandore but was repelled by Chunda.
L318: Chunda and Zaffar entered into a tribute with Zaffar but Chunda later counter-attacked Zaffar and captured Sambhar and Ajmer.^{【227†[30]】} Chunda's aggressive policy earned him enmity with his neignbours, namely Bhati Rao of Pugal, the Sankhlas of Janglu and the governor of Multan, Khidar Khan. These collectively invaded Nagaur. Chunda died defending Nagaur in 1428.^{【228†[31]】}
L319: ### Ranmal
L320: 
L321: [【229†edit】]
L322: Towards the end of his life, he promised his throne to his younger son Kanha which angered his eldest son Ranmal who sought refuge in 【94†Mewar】. Ranmals sister Hansa Bai had been married to 【230†Rana Lakha of Mewar】 so he was able to become a powerful member of the Mewar court.^{【231†[32]】} Karan died soon in the same year and Ranmal was able to gain the throne of Marwar.^{【232†[33]】} Ranmal attacked Bhati, Pugal and Jalore chiefs in his 10 year reign.
L323: Ranmal was called to help rule Mewar in 1433 when his sister Hansa Bai's son Mokal was assassinated. He helped rule the kingdom but his power was not received well by the Sisodia clan. 【233†Rana Kumbha】, son of Mokal, got him assassinated in 1438.^{【234†[34]】}
L324: ### Rao Jodha
L325: 
L326: [【235†edit】]
L327: 
L328: 【122†Jodha of Mandore】, one of the greatest Rathores
L329: After Ranmal's assassination, Mewar and Marwar's relations had gotten bitter and Ranmal's son 【122†Rao Jodha】 escaped Mewar and reached its borders. From there he started building his own power.
L330: He recollected allies and defeated his enemies one by one and consolidated power and by 1453, he was able to regain Mandore.^{【236†[35]】}^{【237†[36]】} To end this enmity, Kumbha and Jodha reached settlements and matrimonial alliance was established by marrying Jodha's daughter Shringar Devi to Kumbha's son 【238†Raimal】 in 1459.^{【239†[37]】} It was after this that Marwar was able to become a prosperous kingdom under the rule of Rao Jodha.
L331: Out of the 15 rulers that preceded Jodha, nine died on the battlefield, six of them against foreign armies. Jodha's 【240†son】 himself died after a battle in which he saved 140 women from Afghan raiders.^{【241†[38]】}
L332: 【242†Mehrangarh Fort】, the capital of Marwar for centuries
L333: 
L334: After restoring peace, Jodha constructed a new capital fort on a hilltop 6 miles south of Mandore named 【242†Mehrangarh】. The city came to be known by his name. Thus the city of 【103†Jodhpur】 was settled.^{【243†[39]】} Soon, he expanded his territory greatly. One of his sons, Bika captured Janglu which he named 【244†Bikaner】 after himself and became its Rao.^{【245†[40]】}
L335: He died in 1489 and was succeeded by his son 【240†Rao Satal】 who died in 1492 who was succeeded by Jodha's second son, 【246†Rao Suja】.^{【247†[41]】} Suja's younger brother Bika laid siege to Jodhpur, demanding a separate kingdom in Bikaner, and in the ensuing negotiations, Bikaner was given to Bika as his independent kingdom.^{【248†[42]】} Suja outlived his eldest son Bagha and died in 1515 and was succeeded by his grandson 【125†Rao Ganga】 in 1515.^{【249†[43]】}
L336: During the rule of Jodha's sons, many chiefs asserted their independence. This led to formations of factions which favored different candidates for succession. When Rao Ganga ascended to the throne, this factionalism led to a civil war like situation because the succession was disputed by another candidate called Biram Deo.^{【250†[44]】}^{【251†[45]】} Soon after Ganga's accession, Biram was expelled from Jodhpur. To avenge this, Biram started looting Jodhpur.
L337: Eventually, with the help of his son Maldeo, Ganga was able to defeat Biram.^{【252†[46]】}
L338: ### Maldeo Rathore
L339: 
L340: [【253†edit】]
L341: 
L342: 【254†Rao Maldev Rathore of Marwar】
L343: Seeing 【255†Rana Sanga】's success, Ganga decided to align Marwar with Mewar and assisted Sanga in war against Sultan of Gujarat in 1517, and 【256†Battle of Bayana】 in 1527 and the 【257†Battle of Khanwa】 in 1527 against 【160†Babur】.
L344: His son 【90†Maldeo Rathore】 participated in the battle and later escorted an unconscious 【255†Rana Sanga】 from the battleground after defeat.^{【258†[47]】}^{【259†[48]】}^{【260†[49]】} After Sanga's death, Maldeo wanted to fill the vacuum of power in Rajputana and felt that his regnant father couldn't fill it.
