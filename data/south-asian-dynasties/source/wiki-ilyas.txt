L143: The Ilyas Shahi dynasty (【66†Middle Bengali】: ইলিয়াস শাহী খান্দান, 【67†Classical Persian】: دودمان الیاس‌شاهی) was the first independent dynasty to set the foundations of the late medieval 【68†Sunni Muslim】 【69†Sultanate of Bengal】. Their rule extended from 1342 to 1487, though interrupted with 【70†interregna】 by their slaves as well as the 【71†House of Ganesha】.^{【72†[1]】}
L144: ## Initial dynasty
L145: 
L146: [【73†edit】]
L147: 
L148: The 【74†Adina Mosque】, once the largest mosque in 【75†the subcontinent】, built by 【76†Sikandar Shah】 in 【64†Hazrat Pandua】.
L149: 
L150: The ancestors of Ilyas Shah originated from 【57†Sistan】, and according to Syed A M R Haque, arrived in 【75†the subcontinent】 as Muslim missionaries and the family were granted 【77†jagirs】 in Bengal in the year 1227. Bengal was under the 【78†Delhi Sultanate】 at the time.
L151: During the governorship of 【79†Izz al-Din Yahya】 in 【63†Satgaon】, 【58†Shamsuddin Ilyas Shah】 took service under him. Following Yahya's death in 1338, Ilyas Shah took control of Satgaon and declared himself as a Sultan, independent of Delhi. He then waged a campaign, defeating both the Sultans 【80†Alauddin Ali Shah】 and 【81†Ikhtiyaruddin Ghazi Shah】 of 【82†Lakhnauti】 and 【83†Sonargaon】 respectively by 1342.
L152: This led to the foundation of Bengal as a single political entity and the start of the 【54†Bengal Sultanate】 and its first dynasty, the Ilyas Shahi.
L153: After Shamsuddin's death, his son 【76†Sikandar Shah】 ascended the throne. Sikandar ruled for the next 30 years and built the 【74†Adina Masjid】 in Pandua in 1368 and Kotwali Darwaza in 【65†Gauḍa】. 【84†Ghiyasuddin Azam Shah】, son of Sikandar Shah succeeded the throne and established friendly relationships with the 【85†Ming Empire】 of China and encouraged trade. During his reign, 【86†Ma Huan】, a Chinese traveller visited Bengal.
L154: ## Instability
L155: 
L156: [【87†edit】]
L157: 
L158: In 1415, political confusion and weakness of the Ilyas Shahi dynasty led to 【88†Saifuddin Hamza Shah】's rule being overthrown by slave 【61†Shihabuddin Bayazid Shah】's family and the 【71†House of Ganesha】 shortly after.^{【89†[2]】} Ganesha's son Jadu embraced Islam and assumed the title of 【90†Jalal ad-Din Muhammad Shah】. He was succeeded by his son, 【91†Shamsuddin Ahmad Shah】. Ahmad was killed by his nobles in 1436 in an attempt to restore the Ilyas Shahi dynasty.
L159: ## Restored dynasty
L160: 
L161: [【92†edit】]
L162: After the death of Shamsuddin Ahmad, Two ephemeral rulers: Qutbuddin Azam Shah and Ghiyasuddin Nusrat Shah ruled Eastern Bengal for a brief period and struck coins in AH 837 (1434 CE). Another ruler, Siraj-al Din Sikandar Shah is only known from his undated coins.
L163: It is not known if any of them were related to Shamsuddin Ahmad Shah.^{【93†[3]】} In any case, the rule of the Ilyas Shahi dynasty was restored by 【94†Mahmud Shah】, a descendant of Shamsuddin Ilyas Shah, who ascended the throne in 1436 as Nasiruddin Mahmud Shah. In 1487, the last ruler of this dynasty 【59†Jalal ad-Din Fatih Shah】 was killed by his 【95†Habshi】 commander of the palace guards, 【96†Shahzada Barbak】, who ascended the throne under the title, Sultan Barbak Shah.
L164: With this, the Ilyas Shahi dynasty's rule over Bengal came to an end.^{【97†[4]】}
