L261: The Maratha Empire,^{【185†[a]】} also referred to as the Maratha Confederacy, was an 【186†early modern】 【187†polity】 in the 【188†Indian subcontinent】. For most of its existence, it comprised the realms of the 【159†Peshwa】 and four major independent 【189†Maratha states】^{【190†[14]】}^{【191†[15]】} under the nominal leadership of the former and nominal loyalty to the 【157†Chhatrapatis】 who were successors of 【164†Shivaji】.
L262: The Marathas were a 【148†Marathi】-speaking warrior group from the western 【192†Deccan Plateau】 (present-day 【193†Maharashtra】) that rose to prominence under leadership of 【164†Shivaji】 (17th century), who revolted against the 【194†Bijapur Sultanate】 and the 【180†Mughal Empire】, establishing "【195†Hindavi Swarajya】" (lit.
L263: (literal translation) 'self-rule of Hindus').^{【196†[16]】}^{【197†[17]】} The religious attitude of 【198†Emperor Aurangzeb】 estranged 【199†non-Muslims】, and the 【200†Maratha insurgency】 came at a great cost for his men and treasury.^{【201†[18]】}^{【202†[19]】} The Maratha government also included warriors, administrators, and other nobles from other 【203†Marathi】 groups.^{【204†[20]】} Shivaji's monarchy, referred to as the Maratha Kingdom,^{【205†[21]】} expanded into a large realm in the 18th century under the leadership of Peshwa 【206†Bajirao I】.
L264: Marathas from the time of 【207†Shahu I】 recognised the 【208†Mughal emperor】 as their nominal 【209†suzerain】, similar to other contemporary Indian entities, though in practice, 【210†Mughal politics】 were largely controlled by the Marathas between 1737 and 1803.^{【211†[b]】}^{【212†[22]】}^{【213†[23]】}^{【214†[c]】}^{【215†[25]】}^{【216†[26]】}^{【217†[d]】}
L265: After Aurangzeb's death in 1707, Shivaji's grandson 【207†Shahu】 under the leadership of Peshwa Bajirao revived Maratha power and confided a great deal of authority to the Bhat family, who became hereditary 【159†peshwas】 (【218†prime ministers】). After he died in 1749, they became the effective rulers. The leading Maratha families – Scindia, Holkar, Bhonsle, and Gaekwad – extended their conquests in northern and central India and became more independent.
L266: The Marathas' rapid expansion was halted with the 【219†defeat of Panipat】 in 1761, at the hands of the 【220†Durrani Empire】.
L267: However the Marathas managed to retake most of their lost territories ten years later under the leadership of Peshwa 【221†Madhavrao I】.^{【222†[28]】} His death eventually marked the end of Peshwa’s effective authority over other chiefs in the empire.^{【223†[29]】}^{【224†[30]】}^{【225†[31]】} After he was defeated by the Holkar dynasty in 1802, the Peshwa 【161†Baji Rao II】 sought protection from the British 【226†East India Company】, whose intervention destroyed the confederacy by 1818 after the 【227†Second】 and 【172†Third Anglo-Maratha Wars】.
L268: The structure of the Maratha state was that of a confederacy of four rulers under the leadership of the Peshwa at 【147†Poona】 (now Pune) in 【228†western India】.
L269: These were the 【229†Scindia】, the 【230†Gaekwad】 based in 【231†Baroda】, the 【232†Holkar】 based in 【233†Indore】 and the 【234†Bhonsle】 based in 【235†Nagpur】.^{【236†[32]】}^{【237†[33]】} The stable borders of the confederacy after the 【238†Battle of Bhopal】 in 1737 extended from modern-day 【193†Maharashtra】^{【239†[34]】} in the south to 【240†Gwalior】 in the north, to 【241†Orissa】 in the east^{【242†[35]】} or about a third (【243†2.5 millions km^{2}】) of the subcontinent.^{【244†[36]】}
L270: ## Nomenclature
L271: 
L272: The Maratha Empire is also referred to as the Maratha Confederacy. Historian 【245†Barbara Ramusack】 notes, "neither term is fully accurate since one implies a substantial degree of centralisation and the other signifies some surrender of power to a central government and a longstanding core of political administrators".^{【246†[37]】}
L273: Historian 【247†Stewart Gordon】 argues against using the term "【248†empire】" in the same sense as the 【249†Roman】 and 【180†Mughal】 empires since the Maratha polity lacked a grand imperial architecture, as well as the term "【250†confederacy】" because it implies long-term stable power-sharing, which did not characterise the Maratha state. Instead, power dynamics within the Maratha leadership frequently changed, sometimes as often as each decade.^{【251†[38]】}
L274: 16th and 17th century texts occasionally used terms such as the "Indian Confederacy" or the "Hindu Confederacy" when referring to the coalition of the Maratha chiefs.^{【252†[39]】}^{【253†[40]】}^{【254†[41]】} Although at present, the word 【255†Maratha】 refers to a traditionally Marathi peasantry group, in the past the word has been used to describe all 【203†Marathi people】.^{【256†[42]】}^{【257†[43]】}
L275: ## History
L276: ### Shivaji and his descendants
L277: 
L278: Early Maratha conquests, in Shivaji's and Shahji's time Portrait of 【164†Shivaji】, in the 【258†Rijksmuseum】, Netherland.
L279: 【164†Shivaji】 (1630–1680) was a Maratha aristocrat of the 【259†Bhonsle】 clan and was the founder of the Maratha state.^{【260†[44]】} Shivaji led a resistance against the 【261†Sultanate of Bijapur】 in 1645 by winning the fort Torna, followed by many more forts, placing the area under his control and establishing 【195†Hindavi Swarajya】 (self-rule of Hindu people^{【197†[17]】}).
L280: He created an independent Maratha state with 【262†Raigad】 as its capital^{【263†[45]】} and successfully fought against the Mughals to defend his kingdom. He was crowned as 【157†Chhatrapati】 (sovereign) of the new Maratha Kingdom in 1674.^{【264†[46]】}
L281: The Maratha dominion under him comprised about 4.1% of the subcontinent, but it was spread over large tracts. At the time of his death,^{【260†[44]】} it was reinforced with about 300 forts, and defended by about 40,000 cavalries, and 50,000 soldiers, as well as naval establishments along the west coast. Over time, the kingdom would increase in size and heterogeneity;^{【265†[47]】} by the time of his grandson's rule, and later under the Peshwas in the early 18th century, it became a vast realm.^{【266†[48]】}
L282: Shivaji had two sons: 【267†Sambhaji】 and 【268†Rajaram】, who had different mothers and were half-brothers. In 1681, Sambhaji succeeded to the crown after his father's death and resumed his expansionist policies. Sambhaji had earlier defeated the 【269†Portuguese】 and 【270†Chikka Deva Raya】 of 【271†Mysore】. To nullify the alliance between his rebel son, Akbar, and the Marathas,^{【272†[49]】} Mughal Emperor 【198†Aurangzeb】 headed south in 1681.
L283: With his entire imperial court, administration and an army of about 500,000 troops, he proceeded to expand the Mughal empire, gaining territories such as the sultanates of Bijapur and 【273†Golconda】. During the eight years that followed, Sambhaji led the Marathas successfully against the Mughals.^{【274†[50]】}
L284: In early 1689, Sambhaji called his commanders for a strategic meeting at 【275†Sangameshwar】 to consider an onslaught on the Mughal forces. In a meticulously planned operation, Ganoji and Aurangzeb's commander, Mukarrab Khan, attacked Sangameshwar when Sambhaji was accompanied by just a few men. Sambhaji was ambushed and captured by the Mughal troops on 1 February 1689.
L285: He and his advisor, 【276†Kavi Kalash】, were taken to Bahadurgad by the imperial army, where 【277†they were executed】 by the Mughals on 21 March 1689.^{【278†[51]】} Aurangzeb had charged Sambhaji with attacks by Maratha forces on 【279†Burhanpur】.^{【280†[52]】}
L286: Upon Sambhaji's death, his half-brother Rajaram ascended the throne. The Mughal siege of Raigad continued, and he had to flee to 【281†Vishalgad】 and then to 【145†Gingee】 for safety. From there, the Marathas raided Mughal territory, and many forts were recaptured by Maratha commanders such as 【282†Santaji Ghorpade】, 【283†Dhanaji Jadhav】, 【284†Parshuram Pant Pratinidhi】, Shankaraji Narayan Sacheev and Melgiri Pandit. In 1697, Rajaram offered a truce, which was later rejected by Aurangzeb.
L287: Rajaram died in 1700 at 【285†Sinhagad】. His widow, 【286†Tarabai】, assumed control in the name of her son 【287†Shivaji II】.^{【288†[53]】}
L288: A portrait of 【267†Sambhaji】, son of 【164†Shivaji】, and the second Maratha ruler.
L289: After Aurangzeb died in 1707, 【207†Shahu】, the son of Sambhaji (and grandson of Shivaji), was released by 【289†Bahadur Shah I】, the new Mughal emperor. However, his mother was kept a hostage of the Mughals to ensure that Shahu adhered to the release conditions. Upon release, Shahu immediately claimed the Maratha throne and challenged his aunt Tarabai and her son. The spluttering Mughal-Maratha war became a three-cornered affair.
L290: This resulted in two rival seats of government being set up in 1707 at Satara and 【290†Kolhapur】 by Shahu and Tarabai respectively.
