L151: The Soomra dynasty (【52†Sindhi】: سومرا گهراڻو, 【96†romanized】: Sūmrā Gharāṇō)^{【97†[4]】} was a late medieval dynasty of 【87†Sindh】, and at times adjacent regions, founded by the 【98†Soomro】 tribe, in modern-day 【99†Pakistan】.^{【100†[5]】}
L152: ## Sources
L153: The only extant source is the Diwan-i Farruhi, a Persian chronicle by Abul-Hasan Ali describing 【101†Mahmud of Ghazni】's invasion (1025 AD) of 【102†Mansura】, the erstwhile capital of Sindh.^{【103†[6]】} Contemporary coinage from Sindh is scarce and of poor quality with offset flans — while some of them can be read to contain the name of 【104†al-Zahir li-i'zaz Din Allah】 and 【105†al-Mustansir Billah】, the 【106†Fatimid caliphs】 from 1021 until 1094, thereafter, they lack the name of the issuer and cannot evidence the dynasty.^{【107†[7]】}
L154: ## History
L155: ### Establishment
L156: The early history of Soomras is unclear. Ali describes the flight and eventual death by drowning of Hafif (var. Khafif), then-ruler of Sindh, during the faceoff with Mahmud but does not specify whether he was the last Habbarid or first Soomra.^{【103†[6]】}^{【108†[a]】} Later chroniclers like 【109†Ali ibn al-Athir】 (c. late 12th c.) and 【110†Ibn Khaldun】 (c.
L157: late 14th c.) attributed the fall of Habbarids to Mahmud of Ghazni, lending credence to the argument of Hafif being the last Habbarid.^{【103†[6]】} The Soomras appear to have established themselves as a regional power in this vacuum.^{【103†[6]】}^{【111†[8]】}
L158: According to 【112†André Wink】, the Soomras were a dynasty of local origin, later claiming to be 【113†Rajputs】 as well as 【114†Arabs】.^{【115†[9]】}^{【116†[10]】} They have been retrospectively claimed to be 【117†Parmar】 Rajputs.^{【118†[11]】} In 【119†Ain-i-Akbari】 (16th century) the Soomra dynasty is mentioned as of Rajput lineage.^{【120†[12]】} Some of them were adherents of 【121†Isma'ilism】 — Arab travelers held them to be 【122†Qarmatians】, and correspondence with the Fatimid caliph, al-Mustansir Billah has been located.^{【111†[8]】}
L159: ### Territory
L160: 
L161: The 【57†Ghurids】 and 【60†Ghaznavids】 continued to rule parts of Sindh, across the eleventh and early twelfth century, alongside Soomras.^{【103†[6]】} The precise delineations have yet to be discovered, but the Soomras were probably centered in lower Sindh.^{【103†[6]】} One of their kings Shimuddin Chamisar had submitted to 【123†Iltutmish】, the 【124†Sultan of Delhi】, and was allowed to continue as a vassal.^{【125†[13]】}
