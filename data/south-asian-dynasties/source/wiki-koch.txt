L176: 【102†Bhutan】
L177: The Koch dynasty (【103†/k ɒ tʃ/】; 1515–1949) ruled parts of eastern 【104†Indian subcontinent】 in present-day 【105†Assam】 and 【106†Bengal】. 【88†Biswa Singha】 established power in the erstwhile 【63†Kamata Kingdom】 which had emerged from the decaying 【107†Kamarupa Kingdom】.^{【108†[7]】}^{【109†[8]】} The dynasty came to power by overthrowing the 【95†Baro-Bhuyans】 of the region, who previously revolted against the rule established by 【110†Alauddin Husain Shah】.
L178: The dynasty split into three among the descendants of Biswa Singha's three sons: two antagonistic branches 【111†Koch Bihar】 and 【112†Koch Hajo】 and a third branch at Khaspur. Koch Bihar aligned with the 【100†Mughals】 and the Koch Hajo branch broke up into various sub-branches under the 【98†Ahom kingdom】. Koch Bihar became a 【113†princely state】 during 【114†British rule】 and was absorbed after Indian independence.
L179: The third branch at Khaspur (Khaspur kingdom) was absorbed completely into the 【101†Kachari kingdom】. 【115†Raikat】 is a collateral branch of the Koch dynasty that claim descent from the Sisya Singha, the brother of Biswa Singha.
L180: ## Etymology
L181: 
L182: [【116†edit】]
L183: 
L184: The name 【117†Koch】 denotes a matrilineal ethnic group to which Biswa Singha's mother belonged;^{【118†[9]】} and the king as well as most of the population of the kingdom (【63†Kamata Kingdom】) belonged to the Koch community.^{【119†[10]】}

L186: ## History
L187: 
L188: [【120†edit】]
L189: ### Historical background
L190: 
L191: [【121†edit】]
L192: 
L193: Main article: 【63†Kamata Kingdom】
L194: After the fall of the 【122†Pala dynasty】, a 【123†Bengali】 dynasty from 【124†Varendra】, the kingdom fractured into different domains in the 12th century.
L195: 【125†Sandhya】, a ruler of Kamarupanagara (present-day 【126†North Guwahati】) moved his capital further west to present-day 【127†North Bengal】 in the middle of the 13th century and the domain he ruled over came to be called 【128†Kamata kingdom】.^{【129†[11]】} The buffer region, between the eastern kingdoms and Kamata was the domain of the 【95†Baro-Bhuyans】 chieftains.
L196: 【110†Alauddin Husain Shah】 of 【130†Gaur】 removed 【131†Nilambar】 of the 【132†Khen dynasty】 in 1498, occupied Kamata and placed his son 【133†Danyal Husayn】 in charge. Within a few years the Baro-Bhuyans—led by one Harup Narayan of the 【134†Brahmaputra valley】—defeated, captured, and executed Daniyal, and the region lapsed into Bhuyan confederate rule.^{【135†[12]】}
L197: #### Emergence of the Koch power
L198: 
L199: [【136†edit】]
L200: It was in this context that a number of independent 【117†Koch】 tribes were united under a leader named Hajo, who occupied 【137†Rangpur】 and 【138†Kamrup】.^{【139†[13]】} The Koches, spreading towards the southern plains, were able to ally with other tribal groups.^{【140†[14]】} Among various factors, the shift from 【141†slash-and-burn】 cultivation to settled cultivation and the breakdown of tribal clan-based relations are given as factors that contributed to the growth of Koch power.^{【142†[15]】}
L201: As part of these alliances Hajo's daughter Hira married Hariya Mandal, a member of the 【143†Mech】 tribe from Chiknagram in present-day 【144†Kokrajhar district】,^{【145†[16]】} though these ethnic identities are difficult to discern since there were frequent intermarriages.^{【146†[17]】}^{【147†[18]】} Bisu, born to Hariya and Hira,^{【148†[19]】} acquired the political legacy of his grandfather Hajo^{【149†[20]】} and established himself as the chief of the eastern branch of the Koches in the Khuntaghat region (present-day 【144†Kokrajhar district】 of Assam).^{【150†[21]】}^{【151†[22]】} It is thought that Bisu fought under the leadership of the Bhuyans as a landlord against the occupation of 【128†Kamata kingdom】 by 【110†Alauddin Husain Shah】 and thus learned their military tactics.^{【152†[23]】}

L205: ### Consolidation of power under Bisu
L206: 
L207: [【156†edit】]
L208: 
L209: He sought the alliance of tribal chiefs,^{【157†[25]】}^{【158†[26]】} against the more powerful Baro-Bhuyans and began his campaign around 1509.^{【159†[27]】} Successively, he defeated the Bhuyans of Ouguri, Jhargaon, Karnapur, Phulaguri, 【64†Bijni】 and 【160†Pandunath】 (Pandu, in 【161†Guwahati】).^{【162†[28]】} He was particularly stretched by the Bhuyan of Karnapur, and could defeat him only by a stratagem during 【163†Bihu】.
L210: In some records Bisu moved his capital from Chikana to Hingulabas (near present-day 【164†Samuktala】) and then finally to Kamatapur (now called 【68†Gosanimari】) which is just a few miles southeast of the present-day 【69†Cooch Behar】 town^{【165†[29]】}—but since these movements were recorded much after the events the dates and rulers associated with these movements are not expected to be accurate and these movements represent the gradual movement of Koch power towards the southern plains of the Brahmaputra Valley.^{【166†[30]】} After subjugating the petty rulers, he announced himself the king of Kamata bounded on the east by 【167†Barnadi River】 and on the west by the 【168†Karatoya River】^{【169†[31]】} in the year 1515.^{【170†[32]】}
L211: The Koch dynasty in Kamata was one of several tribal formations that developed into statehoods around 15th century in 【171†northeast India】—【98†Ahom】, 【172†Chutia】, 【173†Dimasa】, 【174†Tripura】, 【175†Manipur】, etc.^{【176†[33]】}
L212: ### Sanskritisation
L213: 
L214: [【177†edit】]
L215: At his coronation Bisu adopted Hinduism and the name Biswa Singha.^{【178†[34]】}^{【179†[35]】} Nevertheless, he retained the Koch identity of his mother discarding the ethnic identity of his father.^{【180†[36]】} Later, Brahman pundits created a legend that lord 【181†Siva】 was the father of Biswa Singha to give 【182†legitimacy】 to his rule^{【183†[37]】} and conferred on him the status of the 【184†Kshatriya】 varna.^{【185†[38]】} According to the legend constructed at the time of coronation, Bisu was son of Siva and his tribe either the Koch or Mech people were Kshatriyas who ran away from the fear of extermination by the Brahman sage 【186†Parashurama】 and took shelter in 【187†Western Assam】 and 【127†Northern Bengal】 and later disguised themselves as 【188†Mlechchas】.^{【189†[39]】}
L216: This process of hinduisation was much slower in the lower strata of the society,^{【190†[40]】} the king Biswa Singha with his tribal origin claimed Rajbanshi kshatriya status,^{【191†[41]】} the lower class Koch took this name after the 18th century.^{【192†[42]】}
L217: ### Zenith
L218: 
L219: [【193†edit】]
L220: Biswa Singha's two sons, Naranarayan and Shukladhwaj (【194†Chilarai】), the king and the commander-in-chief of the army respectively, took the kingdom to its zenith.
L221: During the reign of Nara Narayan, 【111†Koch Behar】 saw the propagation of 【195†eksarana-namadharma】 by 【196†Sankardev】 along with his two disciples 【197†Madhavdeva】 and 【198†Damodardev】,^{【199†[43]】}^{【200†[44]】} which helped brought a cultural renaissance to the kingdom.^{【201†[45]】} The spread of this new religious movement was initially resisted by the Koch, Mech and Kachari people residing in the Koch-Kamata kingdom,^{【202†[46]】}^{【85†[6]】} for which Nara Narayan made an official order to recognise the different religious practices of the people residing in the kingdom,^{【203†[47]】} though by the end of the 18th century, the masses of the Koch population had absorbed considerable Hindu content.^{【204†[48]】}^{【205†[49]】}
L222: Later, Nara Narayan made Raghudev, the son of Chilarai, the governor of 【112†Koch Hajo】, the eastern portion of the country. After the death of Nara Narayan, Raghudev declared independence. The division of the Kamata kingdom into 【111†Koch Bihar】 and 【112†Koch Hajo】 was permanent.

L267: ### Rulers of Darrang
L268: 
L269: [【215†edit】]
L270: The 【100†Mughal】 【216†Subah】, in alliance with Lakshmi Narayan of Koch Bihar, attacked Parikshit Narayan of 【112†Koch Hajo】 in 1612. Koch Hajo, bounded by Sankosh River in the west and Barnadi river in the east, was occupied by the end of that year. Parikshit Narayan was sent to 【217†Delhi】 for an audience with the Mughal Emperor, but his brother Balinarayan escaped and took refuge in the 【98†Ahom kingdom】.
L271: The region to the east of Barnadi and up to the Bharali river was under the control of some 【95†Baro-Bhuyan】 chieftains, but they were soon removed by the Mughals. In 1615 the Mughals, under Syed Hakim and Syed Aba Bakr, attacked the Ahoms but were repelled back to the Barnadi river. The Ahom king, 【218†Prataap Singha】, then established Balinarayan as a vassal in the newly acquired region between Barnadi and 【219†Bharali】 rivers, and called it 【220†Darrang】.
L272: Balinarayan's descendants continued to rule the region as a tributary to the 【98†Ahom kingdom】 till it was annexed by the 【221†British】 in 1826.^{【222†[51]】}

L312: ### Rulers of Khaspur
L313: 
L314: [【227†edit】]
L315: The 【228†Barak valley】 was obtained by 【194†Chilarai】 in 1562^{【229†[52]】} from the 【174†Twipra kingdom】 during his expedition when he subjugated most of the major rulers in 【171†Northeast India】 and established the Khaspur state with a garrison at Brahmapur, that eventually came to be called Khaspur (Brahmapur→Kochpur→Khaspur).
L316: The Koch rule began with the appointment of Kamal Narayan (step-brother of Chilarai and Naranarayan) as the Dewan a couple of years after the establishment of the garrison.^{【230†[53]】} Kamalnarayan established eighteen clans of Koch families that took hereditary roles in the state of Khaspur and who came to be known as Dheyans (after Dewan).^{【231†[54]】} The independent rule of the Khaspur rulers ended in 1745 when it merged with the 【232†Kachari kingdom】.^{【229†[52]】}
