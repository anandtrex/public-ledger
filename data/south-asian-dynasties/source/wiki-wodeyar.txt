L137: House of Wadiyar
L138: ---

L140: Country  | 【75†Kingdom of Mysore】
L141: Founded  | 11 April 1399 (1399-04-11)^{【76†[1]】}
L142: Founder  | 【77†Yaduraya Wodeyar】
L143: Current head  | 【78†Yaduveer Krishnadatta Chamaraja Wadiyar】

L147: The Wadiyar dynasty (Kannada: 【82†[(ʷ)oɖejɐru]】), also referred to as the Wadiyars of Mysore (also spelt Wodeyar/Odeyar), is a 【83†late-medieval】 【84†Indian】 royal family of former 【80†maharajas of Mysore】^{【85†[2]】} from the 【86†Urs clan】 originally based in 【87†Mysore】 city. They assumed the title ‘Wodeyar,’ which means ‘Lord’ in 【88†Kannada】.^{【89†[3]】}^{【90†[4]】}
L148: As 【80†Maharajas of Mysore】, the Wadiyars ruled the 【75†Kingdom of Mysore】 from the late 1300s until 1950. Members of the Wadiyar dynasty and the Urs clan have also been royal advisers as 【91†dewans】 to their reigning siblings, cousins, nephews, or distant relatives.^{【85†[2]】} Some members have also commanded army divisions as dalvoys (commander-in-chief) for their reigning monarch.
L149: During the late 14th century, the family was originally poleygars (【88†Kannada】 for garrison) defending the regions in and around Mysore town for the 【92†Vijayanagara Empire】, their feudal overlords.
L150: With the 【93†fall and decline of the empire】 in the 17th century, the Wadiyars declared independence when 【94†Timmaraja Wodeyar II】 seized the nearby town of 【95†Srirangapattana】, the seat of Tirumala, 【96†Sriranga II】's viceroy, in 1610.^{【97†[5]】} Between 1766 and 1799, when 【98†Hyder Ali】 and 【99†Tipu】 dictated the kingdom, the Wadiyar rulers as maharajas were largely nominal without any actual powers.^{【100†[6]】} After 【101†Tipu's execution】 in 1799, the 【102†East India Company】 which governed 【103†large territories in India】 at that time restored the kingdom back to the Wadiyars under a 【104†subsidiary alliance】.
L151: After India's 【105†independence】 from the Crown, the ruling Maharaja 【106†Jayachamaraja Wadiyar】 ceded the kingdom to the newly formed 【107†Dominion of India】.^{【108†[7]】}
L152: ## Name
L153: 
L154: In 【88†Kannada】, the common noun odeyaru (【109†Kannada】: ಒಡೆಯರು oḍeyaru) is the plural form of odeya (ಒಡೆಯ) which means lord or lordship. The first poleygar and raja of Mysore, 【77†Yaduraya】, assumed the term as his titular proper noun.
L155: Members of the Wadiyar dynasty hail from the 【86†Urs】 clan; upon adoption or by heredity, they assume the title Wadiyar and their immediate family therewith, the latest instance of its happening with the present head of the family 【78†Yaduveer Wadiyar】 upon his coronation in 2015.^{【110†[8]】}^{【111†[9]】}^{【112†[10]】}
L156: ## History
L157: 
L158: The Wadiyars claim descent from the 【113†Hindu】 deity 【114†Krishna】. Legend has it that they arrived from 【115†Dvārakā】.^{【116†[11]】} However, historians like P. V. Nanjaraj Urs, Shyam Prasad, Nobuhiro Ota, 【117†David Leeming】, and Aya Ikegame instead suggest that the Wadiyars were local feudal lords who purported 【118†Puranic】 ancestry and claimed to be the direct descendants of the 【119†Lunar Dynasty】.^{【120†[12]】}^{【121†[13]】}^{【122†[14]】}^{【123†[15]】}
L159: ### Vassal fiefdom
L160: 
L161: The Wadiyar dynasty started when 【77†Yaduraya】, a garrison leader (poleygar), was made the prefect of Mysore and the surrounding regions by his overlord 【124†Harihara II】 of the 【92†Vijayanagara Empire】 in 1399. With this, Yaduraya assumed the title Raja and the honorary surname Wadiyar. He and his successors ruled the fiefdom of Mysore as rajas under the vassalage of the 【92†Vijayanagara Empire】 until around 1553.
L162: ### Independent kingdom
L163: The Vijayanagara Empire 【93†disintegrated】 in 1565. With the fall of the empire, Mysore became an independent kingdom, the first independent king being 【94†Timmaraja Wodeyar II】, the great-great-great-grandson of the founding ruler Yaduraya. Thimmaraja's nephew 【125†Raja Wodeyar I】 expanded the borders of the kingdom. In 1610, he moved the capital from Mysore to nearby island town of 【95†Srirangapattana】 on the river 【126†Kaveri】, which provided strategic protection against military attacks.
L164: Raja Wadiya's cousin and successor down the line 【127†Kanthirava Narasaraja I】 expanded the frontiers of the kingdom to 【128†Trichy】 in present-day 【129†Tamil Nadu】. The kingdom reached its peak under Kanthirava's grand-nephew 【130†Devaraja Wodeyar II】, who reformed the administration of the kingdom by dividing it into 18 departments (called chavadis); he also introduced a coherent system of taxation.

L168: ### During Hyder Ali and Tipu
L169: 
L170: From 1760 to 1799, the rule of the Wadiyar dynasty was essentially nominal, with real power firmly in the hands of the Commander-in-chief and later self-proclaimed sultan, 【98†Hyder Ali】, and his son and successor 【99†Tipu】. The two, ruling the sultanate from 【95†Srirangapattana】, expanded the kingdom aggressively.

L177: ### Independent kingdom in a subsidiary alliance
L178: 
L179: After 【101†Tipu's defeat】 in the 【132†Siege of Srirangapattana】 during the 【133†Fourth Anglo-Mysore War】 in 1799, the 【134†British】 restored the Kingdom to the Wadiyars under a subsidiary alliance that required an annual payment as tribute. The capital was moved back to 【87†Mysore】. The four-year-old infant prince 【135†Krishnaraja Wodeyar III】, adopted son of the previous ruler 【136†Chamaraja Wadiyar IX】, was anointed as the 【80†Maharaja of Mysore】.
L180: In 1831, on a specious plea of non-payment by Krishnaraja Wodeyar III, the kingdom was placed under 【137†Mysore Commission】 that lasted from 1831 to 1881. 【138†Mark Cubbon】 and 【139†L. B. Bowring】 were among the well-known commissioners of the period.
L181: In 1868, upon Krishnaraja Wodeyar III's demise, his five-year-old grandson 【140†Chamaraja Wadiyar X】 became the heir to the throne. When in 1881 he attained the age of majority, through an act of parliament, the British once again transferred power back to the Wadiyars. The maharaja changed the English spelling of their royal name from Wodeyar to Wadiyar. He established the 【141†Mysore Representative Assembly】; the first of its kind in 【142†Princely India】.
L182: Chamaraja Wadiyar X's son and successor 【143†Krishnaraja Wadiyar IV】 earned great fame as a saintly king, and his kingdom was hailed as Ramarajya by 【144†Mahatma Gandhi】–as an ideal kingdom comparable to the one ruled by Lord 【145†Rama】.^{【146†[16]】}
L183: Krishnaraja Wadiyar IV's nephew, successor, and the last reigning king of the Wadiyar dynasty, 【106†Jayachamarajendra Wadiyar】, ruled from 1940 until 1950. Upon 【147†India's independence】 from the British crown in 1947, Jayachamarajendra Wadiyar ceded his kingdom to the new provisional 【107†Dominion of India】 but continued as Maharajah until India became a Republic in 1950.

L193: ### Dissolution of the kingdom
L194: 
L195: Jayachamarajendra Wadiyar became the 【153†rajapramukh】 of the renamed 【154†Mysore State】 from 1950 to 1956. After the reorganisation of Indian states on a linguistic basis in 1956, he was appointed 【155†Governor】 of the integrated Mysore State (present 【156†Karnataka State】), a post which he held until 1964. He was then appointed 【157†Governor of Madras】 (now 【129†Tamil Nadu】) for two years.
L196: The 【158†Indian Constitution】 continued to recognise him as the Maharajah of Mysore until 1971, when titles and privy purses of maharajas were abolished by the 【159†Government of India】 under 【160†Indira Gandhi】. The maharajah died in 1974.
L197: 
L198: His only son 【161†Srikantadatta Narasimharaja Wadiyar】 became the head of the family; he was a 【162†member of the Indian Parliament】.
L199: Upon Srikantadatta Wadiyar's demise 2013, his widowed wife Devi Pramoda Wadiyar adopted 【78†Yaduveer Wadiyar】, who was anointed as the maharaja of Mysore and the head of the family in 2015.

L206: ## Residences
L207: 
L208: 【165†Mysore Palace】 has been the official residence of the Wadiyars for most of the family's recorded history. Briefly, 【95†Srirangapattana】 was also the seat of the Wadiyars. By the early 1900s, Bangalore had seen significant infrastructural development and had become a secondary residence for the Wadiyars at the 【166†Bangalore Palace】.

L214: ## Alamelamma's Curse
L215: 
L216: With a declining Vijayanagara Empire, in 1610 【125†Raja Wadiyar】 conquered the fort of Srirangapattana from Tirumala, the Vijayanagara viceroy stationed there. Tirumala is said to have retired to the nearby town of 【170†Talakadu】 with his two wives. At about the same time, Tirumala suffered from a terminal disease; his condition deteriorated and he eventually died.
L217: One of his wives was Alamelamma, a staunch devotee of Ranganayaki, the consort of Lord 【171†Ranganatha】, the presiding deity of the famous Adi-Ranga temple in the island fortress of 【95†Srirangapatna】. Alamelamma had a large amount of jewellery, including a fine nose ring studded with a large pearl. As a widow, customs forbade jewellery on her. She frequently lent the jewels to the temple of Ranganayaki.
L218: Every Tuesdays and Fridays, Ranaganayaki's idol would be decorated with the jewellery, returned to Alamelamma's safe custody for rest of the week.
L219: On one instance, the temple requested the king's soldiers to fetch the jewels from Alamelamma as has become a practice. An approaching king's emissaries, headed by Chief Courtier Ramanath Molahalli, seems to have scared her off.
L220: To escape an ill-presumed imminent wrath of the king, she ran over to a cliff overlooking the Kaveri river into the whirlpool and cursed before plunging to her own death, "may 【170†Talakadu】 become a barren expanse of land, Malangi turn into a whirlpool, and may kings of Mysore never beget children to all eternity" (【88†Kannada】: ತಲಕಾಡು ಮರಳಾಗಲಿ, ಮಾಲಂಗಿ ಮಡುವಾಗಲಿ, ಮೈಸೂರು ದೊರೆಗಳಿಗೆ ಮಕ್ಕಳಿಲ್ಲದೆ ಹೋಗಲಿ; transliteration: talakāḍu maral̤āgali, mālaṃgi maḍuvāgali, maisūru dŏrĕgal̤igĕ makkal̤illadĕ hogali).
L221: ### Alamelamma's idol
L222: 
L223: Learning of this accident, the king felt repentant and had an idol of Alamelamma made in gold, installed it in the palace, and worshipped it as a deity. To this day, the idol can be found in 【165†Mysore Palace】. The king's only surviving son, Narasaraja Wadiyar, died (believed to be an effect of the curse in folklore).
L224: The 【131†Dasara】 festivities inside the palace end on the evening of 【172†Navarathri】 with a formal pooja to Alamelamma. Another interesting part of the story is that the Alamelamma temple is under the care of her legal heirs. Strangely, these priests/caretakers appear to be cursed by the same curse, following the same pattern afflicting the Wadiyars.
L225: ### Talakadu and Malangi
L226: 
L227: Lord Vishnu Temple under sand dunes in 【170†Talakadu】, Karnataka
L228: Talakadu and Malangi are two small towns near 【173†Tirumakudalu Narasipura】 on the banks of the Kaveri where the river takes a bend. To date, most parts of Talakadu lie under sand, and the village of Malangi is slowly eroding due to whirlpools.^{【174†[17]】}^{【175†[18]】} Talakadu's temples lie buried in a vast expanse of sand and are dug up and exposed every 12 years. At Malangi, on the other hand, the river is at its deepest.
L229: Whether these phenomena appeared only after Alamelamma's curse in 1610 is a matter of conjecture.
L230: ## Family tree of the Wadiyars
L231: ### Direct lineage from Yaduraya
L232: Raja Yaduraya is recorded as the founding 【80†ruler】 of the 【75†Kingdom of Mysore】. The sixteenth maharaja 【176†Krishnaraja Wodeyar I】 was the last direct male lineage of Raja Yaduraja.^{【177†[19]】} However, Krishnaraja Wodeyar I's successor was his adopted son, 【178†Maharaja Chamaraja Wodeyar VII】 from the Ankanahalli Urs branch. After him, Maharaja Chamaraja Wodeyar III's second daughter Rajakumari Chikkadevi's family with the Bettada Kote Urs branch takes over the monarchy.
