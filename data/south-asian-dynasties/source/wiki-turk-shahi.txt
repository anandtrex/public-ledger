L169: The Turk Shahis were a dynasty【132†Hephthalite】 origin,^{【133†[3]】} that ruled from 【107†Kabul】 and 【134†Kapisa】 to 【135†Gandhara】 in the 7th to 9th centuries CE.^{【68†[1]】}^{【136†[4]】}^{【137†[5]】} They may have been of 【138†Khalaj】 ethnicity.^{【139†[6]】}^{【140†[7]】}^{【141†[8]】} The Gandhara territory may have been bordering the 【142†Kashmir kingdom】 to the east.^{【143†[9]】} From the 560s, the Western Turks had gradually expanded southeasterward from 【144†Transoxiana】, and occupied 【145†Bactria】 and the 【146†Hindu Kush】 region, forming largely independent polities.^{【147†[10]】} The Turk Shahis may have been a political extension of the neighbouring Western Turk 【148†Yabghus of Tokharistan】.^{【136†[4]】} In the Hindu Kush region, they replaced the 【127†Nezak Huns】 – the last dynasty of Bactrian rulers with origins among the 【149†Xwn】 (Xionite) and/or 【150†Huna】 peoples (who are sometimes also referred to as "Huns" who invaded Eastern Europe during a similar period).^{【136†[4]】}
L170: The Turk Shahis arose at a time when the 【151†Sasanian Empire】 had already been conquered by the 【152†Rashidun Caliphate】. The Turk Shahis then resisted for more than 250 years the eastward expansion of the 【153†Abbasid Caliphate】, until they fell to the Persian 【154†Saffarids】 in the 9th century CE.^{【155†[11]】} The 【156†Ghaznavids】 then finally broke through into India after overpowering the declining succeeding 【157†Hindu Shahis】 and 【158†Gurjaras】.^{【68†[1]】}^{【136†[4]】}
L171: 【159†Kabulistan】 was the heartland of the Turk Shahi domain, which at times included 【160†Zabulistan】 and Gandhara.^{【161†[12]】}
L172: ## Territorial extents
L173: 
L174: [【162†edit】]
L175: The Turks under the 【163†Western Turk】 ruler 【164†Tong Yabghu Qaghan】 crossed the 【146†Hindu Kush】 and occupied 【135†Gandhara】 as far as the 【165†Indus River】 from circa 625 CE.^{【166†[13]】}^{【167†[14]】} Overall, the territory of the Turk Shahi extended from Kapisi to Gandhara, with a Turkic branch becoming independent in Zabulistan at one point.^{【143†[9]】}^{【168†[15]】}^{【139†[6]】} The Gandhara territory may have been bordering the 【169†Kashmir kingdom】 and the 【170†Kanauj kingdom】 to the east.^{【143†[9]】} The Turk Shahi capital of Gandhara, which possibly functioned as a winter capital alternating with the summer capital of Kabul, was 【171†Udabhandapura】.^{【172†[16]】} The Korean pilgrim 【173†Hui Chao】, visiting the area in 723–729 CE, mentioned that these regions were ruled by Turk kings.
L176: ## History
L177: 
L178: [【174†edit】]
L179: ### Establishment: Arab offensive and displacement of the Nezaks
L180: 
L181: [【175†edit】]
L182: The last extant 【176†Nezak】 ruler Ghar-ilchi was recorded as the king of 【177†Jibin】 (former 【178†Kapisi】/ 【159†Kabulistan】) by the 【83†Tang dynasty】 in 653 CE.^{【179†[17]】} He was also likely to be the unnamed ruler who was confirmed as Governor of Jibin under the newly formed Chinese 【111†Anxi Protectorate】 in 661 CE and would broker a peace-treaty with the Arabs, the same year.^{【180†[18]】}^{【181†[19]】} Nonetheless, in 664–665 CE, 【182†Abd al-Rahman ibn Samura】 launched an expedition to reconquer the territories lost during the Caliphate Wars.^{【183†[a]】} Kabul was occupied in 665 CE after a siege of a few months but soon revolted, only to be reoccupied after another year-long siege.^{【184†[b]】} These events mortally weakened the Nezaks though their ruler — not named in sources — was spared upon converting to 【185†Islam】.^{【186†[23]】}
L183: Sometime soon (666/667?), the Nezaks were replaced by the Turk Shahis, first in Zabulistan and then in Kabulistan and Gandhara.^{【187†[24]】} Their ethnic identity remains unclear and the name might be a misnomer.^{【188†[c]】} According to 【189†Hyecho】, who visited the region about 50 years after the events, the first Shahi ruler of Kapisi — named Barha Tegin by 【190†al-Biruni】 — was an usurper, who used to be a military commander in the service of the last Nezak King.^{【191†[26]】}^{【192†[27]】}^{【193†[d]】} Al-Biruni provides a rather legendary account of Barhategin's rise, extrapolating from multiple mythological motifs,^{【194†[e]】} and the precise circumstances surrounding the dawning of the Turk Shahis remain unclear.^{【195†[f]】}
L184: ### Tang Protectorate and vassalage to the Tokhara Yabghus
L185: 
L186: [【196†edit】]
L187: 
L188: The 【197†Bala Hissar fortress】, west Kabul, originally built around the 5th century CE
L189: The Turk Shahis, like the rest of the Western Turks, were nominally part of a protectorate under the Chinese 【83†Tang dynasty】 since circa 658 CE.^{【198†[33]】} The territory of the Turk Shahis was nominally partitioned into several Chinese Commanderies under administration of the 【88†Anxi Protectorate】: the city of Yege (modern 【199†Mihtarlam】) east of Kabul was considered as the seat of a Chinese Commandery for the 【177†Jibin】 country, and named the Xiuxian Commandery (修鮮都督府, Xiūxiān Dūdùfû),^{【180†[18]】} the city of Yan at the border with Gandhara was the seat of the Yuepan Commandery (悅般都督府, Yuèpān Dūdùfû), 【105†Ghazni】 was the seat of the Tiaozhi Commandery (條枝都督府, Tiáozhī Dūdùfû).^{【200†[34]】}^{【201†[35]】}
L190: According to Chinese sources, in particular the chronicles of the 【202†Cefu Yuangui】, the Turks in Kabul were vassals of the 【80†Yabghus of Tokharistan】, who in turn swore allegiance to the Tangs.^{【203†[36]】} A young brother of the 【204†Tokhara Yabghu】 Pantu Nili — named Puluo (僕羅 púluó) in Chinese sources — visited the court of the Tang dynasty in 【205†Xi'an】 in 718 CE and gave an account of the military forces in the Tokharistan region, explaining that "two hundred and twelve kingdoms, governors and prefects" had been recognizing the authority of the Yabghus (specifically mentioning among them that "the king of Zabul rules two hundred thousand soldiers and horses, the king of Kabul two hundred thousand"), since the time of his grandfather, that is, probably since the time of their establishment.^{【203†[36]】}^{【206†[37]】}^{【207†[g]】}^{【208†[h]】}
L191: In about 680–683, the kingdom split into two with the Zunbil fleeing from his brother, the king of Kabul, and approaching Salm b. Ziyad at Amul in Khorasan. In return for him agreeing to acknowledge Salm as his overlord, the Zunbil was allowed to settle down in Amul. Soon he drove his brother out and established himself in Amul. The location of Amul mentioned by Tabari is not certain, 【209†Josef Markwart】 identified it with Zabul.
L192: Tabari however claims the Kabul Shah fled instead from the Zunbil and established himself as an independent king during the reign of Mu'awiya.^{【210†[39]】}^{【211†[40]】}
L193: ### Conflict with the Arabs
L194: 
L195: [【212†edit】]
L196: 
L197: See also: 【213†Muslim conquest of Sistan】, 【214†Muslim conquests of Afghanistan】, and 【215†Muslim conquests in the Indian subcontinent】

L199: The Turk Shahis were affected by the 【215†Muslim conquests in the Indian subcontinent】.
L200: 
L201:   Desert areas (【216†Registan Desert】 and 【217†Thar Desert】)

L203:   【108†Zunbils】
L204: 
L205:   Turk Shahis
L206: 
L207:   【142†Kingdom of Kashmir】
L208: 
L209:   【218†Kingdom of Sindh】 (c. 632– 711 CE)
L210: then, 【72†Caliphal province of Sind】 (712-854 CE)
L211:   【219†Maitraka Kingdom】 (c.475–c.776 CE)
L212: Under 【220†Barha Tegin】, the Shahis mounted a counter-offensive and repulsed the Arab forces after Abd al-Rahman ibn Samura was replaced as 【221†Governor of Sistan】 c.665 CE, taking back lost territory as far as the region of 【222†Arachosia】 and 【104†Kandahar】.^{【223†[41]】}^{【224†[42]】}^{【225†[43]】} The capital was shifted from Kapisa to Kabul.^{【226†[44]】} The Arabs attempted a counter-offensive when 【227†Rabi ibn Ziyad al-Harithi】 assumed the governorship of Sistan in 671 CE, attacking the Turkic "【108†Rutbil】" at 【114†Bost】, and driving him to al-Rukhkhaj (【222†Arachosia】).^{【223†[41]】} Rabi's successor 【228†Ubayd Allah ibn Abi Bakra】 continued the war upon being appointed in 673 CE, leading Rutbil to negotiate a peace treaty for both Kabul and 【229†Zabul】, in which the governor of 【230†Sistan】 acknowledged control of these territories by Rutbil and the King of Kabul.^{【223†[41]】} Little more is known about the rule of Barha Tegin, but many of the early Turk Shahi coins are attributed to him.^{【231†[45]】}
L213: He was succeeded by his son 【232†Tegin Shah】 c.
L214: 680, whose regal title was "【233†Khorasan】 Tegin Shah" meaning "Tegin, King of the East", probably referring to his resistance against the 【234†Umayyad caliph】.^{【225†[43]】}^{【235†[46]】} His territory comprised the area from Kabulistan to Gandhara and initially included Zabulistan, which came to be ruled by Rutbil (Turkic: Iltäbär), his elder brother, who founded the dynasty of the 【108†Zunbils】.^{【235†[46]】} Their relationship was at times antagonistic, but they fought together against Arab incursions.^{【235†[46]】}^{【236†[47]】}
L215: The Arabs again failed to capture Kabul and Zabulistan in 683 CE: their general Abu Ubaida ibn Ziyad was imprisoned in Kabul and the governor of 【237†Sijistan】 【238†Yazid ibn Ziyad】 was killed as he attacked the city.^{【136†[4]】}^{【239†[48]】} In 684–685, Kabul briefly comes under Arab control.^{【239†[48]】} In 698 Ubayd Allah ibn Abi Bakra of the Umayyad Caliphate lead an 'Army of Destruction' against the Zunbils, was defeated and was forced to offer a large tribute, give hostages including three of his sons and take an oath not to invade Zunbil again.^{【240†[49]】} About 700 【241†Ibn al-Ash'ath】 tried again to invade with the 'Peacock Army', but after some initial progress eventually formed a peace treaty with the Turks, and turned around to lead a rebellion against the Umayyad viceroy of the east, 【242†al-Hajjaj ibn Yusuf】.^{【243†[50]】}
L216: Tegin Shah apparently regained complete suzerainty over Zabulistan around 710 CE.^{【235†[46]】} This appears from the accounts in the Chinese chronicles, which relates that the rulers of Zabulistan "subjugated themselves to Jibin (Kabul)", sometime between 710 and 720 CE.^{【244†[51]】} During this period, it seems the Zunbils and the Turk Shahis intermittently accepted, or were forced to accept, payment of taxes to the Arabs, thereby acknowledging some form of political dependence, but resisted fiercely when the Arabs attempted to take a more direct military, political or religious control.^{【245†[52]】}
L217: From 711 CE, the Turk Shahis also had to face a Muslim threat from the southeast, as the campaigns of 【246†Muhammad ibn Qasim】 established the 【72†Caliphal province of Sind】, as far as 【100†Multan】, at the gates of 【247†Punjab】, which would last until 854 CE as an Umayyad and then 【248†Abbasid】 dependency.^{【249†[53]】}
L218: #### Tang dynasty investiture
L219: 
L220: [【250†edit】]
L221: 
L222: In 719/20 CE, the Tegin of Kabulistan (Tegin Shah) and the Iltäbär of Zabulistan (here named "Shiquer") sent a combined embassy to 【251†Xuanzong】, the Chinese Emperor of the Tang dynasty in Xi'an, to obtain confirmation of their thrones.^{【180†[18]】} The Chinese emperor signed an investiture decree, which was returned to the Turk rulers. The official Chinese recognition of the enthronement of Tegin Shah appears in the annals of the 【252†Tangshu】:
L223: Portrait of the Turk Shahis ruler 【232†Tegin Shah】, who received the investiture from the 【251†Tang Emperor】 in 719/720 CE, as "King of 【107†Jibin】, Tegin (ruler) of the 【138†Khalaj】".^{【235†[46]】}^{【180†[18]】}
L224: > In the seventh year of the Kaiyuan reign [719 CE], [Jibin (Kabul) dispatched] envoys to the [Tang] court, who offered up a book of an astrological text, secret medical recipes, together with foreign medecines and other things. An imperial edict was issued to bestow on the king [of Jibin] the title 【138†Geluodazhi】 Tele [for "Tegin"].
L225: 
L226: — 【253†Old Book of Tang】, Book 198.^{【235†[46]】}^{【180†[18]】}^{【254†[54]】}
L227: The word "Geluodazhi" in this extract (Chinese: 葛罗达支, pronounced in 【255†Early Middle Chinese】: kat-la-dat-tcǐe), is thought to be a transliteration of the ethnonym 【138†Khalaj】.^{【256†[55]】} Hence Tegin Shah was described as "Tegin of the Khalaj".^{【256†[55]】} This title also appears on his coinage in 【257†Gupta script】, where he is named "hitivira kharalāča", probably meaning "【258†Iltäbär】 of the Khalaj".^{【256†[55]】}
L228: In 720 CE, the ruler of Zabulistan (謝䫻, Xiėyù) also received the title Gedaluozhi Xielifa (Chinese: 葛達羅支頡利發), Xielifa being the known Chinese transcription of the Turkish "【258†Iltäbär】", hence "Iltäbär of the Khalaj".^{【259†[56]】} This appears in another extract from the Tangshu describing the country of Zabulistan (谢䫻, Xiėyù), mentioning how Zabulistan was a vassal to the Kabul Shah around the same period, and how the Zunbil ruler, named "Shiquer", was also recognized by the Chinese court:^{【260†[57]】}^{【261†[58]】}
