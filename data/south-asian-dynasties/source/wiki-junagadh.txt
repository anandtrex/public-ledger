L163: The State of Junagadh was a 【95†princely state】 in the 【96†Kathiawar Peninsula】 of western India, located in present-day 【93†Gujarat】.
L164: Ruled by the 【97†Babi dynasty】 of Muslim Nawabs since 1654, it was one of the larger princely states in the region firstly within the 【90†Maratha Empire】 and later by 【98†British India】.^{【99†[3]】} In 1947, following the 【100†partition of India】, Junagadh became the subject of a political dispute when its ruler, Nawab Mahabat Khan III, acceded to 【101†Pakistan】 despite the state's predominantly Hindu population and its geographical contiguity with India.^{【102†[4]】} This decision led to unrest and the eventual 【103†intervention】 of the Government of India.
L165: After a plebiscite held in 1948, in which an overwhelming majority voted in favor of accession to 【104†India】, Junagadh was integrated into the Indian Union and later became part of the state of Gujarat.^{【105†[5]】}^{【106†[6]】} Pakistan claims sovereignty over the erstwhile princely state to this day.^{【107†[7]】}^{【108†[8]】}
L166: ## History
L167: 
L168: Junagadh Museum
L169: 
L170: The state of Junagadh was established by the Maharaja of 【109†Parmar】 【110†Rajputs】. Muhammad Sher Khan Babai was the founder of the 【97†Babi Pashtun dynasty】 of Junagarh in 1654. His descendants, the Babi Nawabs of Junagarh, conquered large territories in southern 【111†Saurashtra】.
L171: However, during the collapse of the 【112†Mughal Empire】, the Babis became involved in a struggle with the 【113†Gaekwad dynasty】 of the 【90†Maratha Empire】 over control of 【93†Gujarat】, during the reign of the local Mohammad Mahabat Khanji I. Mohammad Khan Bahadur Khanji I declared independence from the Mughal governor of Gujarat subah, and founded the state of Junagarh in 1730. This allowed the Babi to retain sovereignty of Junagarh and other princely states.
L172: During the reign of his heir Junagarh was a tributary to the Maratha Empire,^{【114†[9]】} until it came under 【115†British suzerainty】 in 1807 under Mohammad Hamid Khanji I,^{【99†[3]】} following the 【116†Second Anglo-Maratha War】.
L173: In 1807, Junagarh became a British protectorate and the 【82†East India Company】 took control of the state. By 1818, the 【91†Saurashtra】 area, along with other 【117†princely states】 of 【118†Kathiawar】, were separately administered under the 【119†Kathiawar Agency】 by 【98†British India】.
L174: In 1947, during the 【100†partition of India】, the last Babi dynasty ruler of the state, 【120†Muhammad Mahabat Khanji III】, decided to accede to the 【101†Dominion of Pakistan】 but 【88†Junagarh was annexed】 by India which was followed by a plebiscite in which the locals voted to stay with India.
L175: ## Koli Rebellion
L176: 
L177: A coat of arms was granted to Muhammed Mahabat Khanji II at the Durbar in Delhi of 1877, used until 1947.
L178: There was a 【121†Koli】 rebellion in Junagarh by Mansa Khant during the reign of 【86†Nawab】 Sher Khan (the first ruler of Junagarh). He revolted against the ongoing 【112†Mughal Rule】, with 【122†Uparkot Fort】 serving as his centre of operations. He made a series of raids into the surrounding villages and cities. Nawab Sher Khan was unsuccessful in suppressing the rebellion. Mansa Khant occupied Uparkot for 13 months and continued to carry out numerous raids mostly in the countryside.
L179: The nawab was assisted by the ruler of 【123†Gondal State】, Thakur Sahib Haloji 【124†Jadeja】 and Arab Jamadar Sheikh Abdullah Zubeidi in his campaign against the rebellion. The combined forces defeated Mansa Khant, captured Uparkot and crushed the rebellion.^{【125†[10]】}^{【126†[11]】}
L180: ## Annexation by India
L181: 
L182: Main article: 【88†Annexation of Junagarh】
L183: 
L184: In 1947, 【127†Shah Nawaz Bhutto】 joined the council of ministers of Nawab 【87†Muhammad Mahabat Khan III】, and in May became his 【128†dewan】 or prime minister.
L185: 
L186: With the 【100†partition of India】 in 1947, the princely states were left by the British to decide whether to accede to one of the newly independent Union of India or Dominion of Pakistan, or become a separate country.^{【129†[12]】}
L187: The Constitutional Advisor to the Nawab, Nabi Baksh, indicated to 【130†Lord Mountbatten】 that he was recommending that Junagarh should join India. However, upon the advice of Dewan Bhutto, on 15 August 1947, the Nawab announced that Junagarh had acceded to Pakistan. On 16 September, the Government of Pakistan accepted the accession.^{【102†[4]】}
L188: India sent its military into Junagarh while the Nawab of Junagarh was in Pakistan and captured the State of Junagarh. The annexation of Junagarh by India led to the Nawab 【87†Muhammad Mahabat Khan III】 of Junagarh (of the erstwhile Babi Nawab dynasty of Junagarh) living in exile in 【131†Sindh】, Pakistan.^{【132†[13]】}
L189: ### Pakistan's claim
L190: 
L191: Pakistan's government has maintained its territorial claim on Junagadh, along with 【133†Manavadar】 and 【134†Sir Creek】 in Gujarat, on its official political maps.^{【135†[14]】}^{【107†[7]】}^{【108†[8]】}^{【136†[15]】} On 10 December 2020, 【137†Sultan Ahmed Ali】 took oath as the Dewan of Junagadh State.^{【138†[16]】}
