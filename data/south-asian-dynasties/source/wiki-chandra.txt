L135: The Chandra dynasty was a 【62†Buddhist】 dynasty, originating from the South-east 【63†Bengal】 region of 【64†Indian subcontinent】, which ruled the 【65†Samatata】 area of Bengal, as well as parts of 【66†Arakan】 Later it was a neighbour to the 【61†Pala Empire】 to the north. Rulers of Chandra kingdom were adherents of 【57†Buddhism】.
L136: The Kings of Chandra dynasty were identified as the kings of Vangaladesha in the Tirumulai inscription of 【67†Chola dynasty】.^{【68†[1]】} The dynasty was founded around the 4th century AD.
L137: ## History
L138: 
L139: [【69†edit】]
L140: 
L141: The Anandachandra Inscription (729 AD) mentions that the Chandra dynasty was established by Dvenchandra (or Mahataing Chandra) in 370 AD. He had assumed the throne after the end of the Annaveta dynasty.^{【70†[2]】} The ye Dhamma inscriptions of the Queen of Niti Chandra are dated to early 6th century AD based on paleographic grounds.^{【71†[3]】}
L142: The Chandra kingdom was one of the last 【62†Buddhist】 strongholds in the 【64†Indian subcontinent】. The kingdom flourished as a center of the Tantric schools of Buddhism. It played a role in the diffusion of 【72†Mahayana】 Buddhism to Southeast Asia.^{【73†[4]】}
L143: During the reign of 【74†Srichandra】 (c 930–975 AD), the administrative centre of the Chandra kingdom was established in 【75†Bikrampur】 in 【76†Vanga】.^{【77†[5]】} He led invasions into 【78†Kamarupa】. The Chandras played an important role in the regional politics and military history of erstwhile 【63†Bengal】.
L144: 
L145: In the reign of King 【79†Govindachandra】, the Chandras were eventually annexed by the 【80†Pala dynasty】 of 【81†Varendra】.^{【77†[5]】}
L146: ## Conquest of Kamarupa
L147: 
L148: [【82†edit】]
L149: 
L150: Verse 12 of the 【83†Sylhet】 plate informs us that 【74†Sricandra】 conquered 【78†Kamrupa】. The description of the land of Kmarupa with its pigeons, monkeys, plantain tress, deers and black aloe-woods, as given in the verse, shows us that the composer was familiar with the country.^{【84†[6]】}
L151: The next verse indicates that his victorious army went beyond the Lohitya(Brahmaputra) river to the north of Gauhati, where flows the puspabhadra river and proceeded through Uttarapatha to the mountainous region.^{【84†[6]】}
L152: ## Maritime relations
L153: 
L154: [【85†edit】]
L155: 
L156: The coastal kingdom had trade networks with states in what is now Myanmar, Thailand, Indonesia and Vietnam. 10th century shipwrecks around the coast of 【86†Java】 provide evidence of maritime links between southeastern Bengal and Southeast Asia. Bronze sculptures may have been imported by the Javanese from the Chandra kingdom in southeastern Bengal. 【87†Arab】 merchants also traded with the kingdom.^{【73†[4]】}
L157: King Anandachandra mentions mission he sent to 【88†Sinhalese King】 Silamegha (【89†Aggabodhi IV】) (727–766)
L158: ## Marital relations
L159: 
L160: [【90†edit】]
L161: 
L162: According to king Anandachandra's (22nd King of this dynasty) inscription, the Chandra dynasty had marital relations with the Tamralipta royal family (【91†Tamluk Royal Family】). The Bengali King Anandachandra of Arakan was married to Princess Dhenda of Tamralipta.^{【92†[7]】}^{【93†[8]】}
L163: ## Archaeology
L164: 
L165: [【94†edit】]
L166: 
L167: There are numerous inscriptions dating from the period of the Chandra dynasty. The three archaeological sites associated with the dynasty include 【75†Bikrampur】 and 【95†Mainamati】 in Bangladesh and 【96†Waithali】 in 【97†Rakhine State】, Myanmar.
L168: ## Education
L169: 
L170: [【98†edit】]
L171: 【99†Chandrapur University】 in Shrihatta was as large as other religious institutions like 【100†Nalanda】, 【101†Taxila】, 【102†Mahasthangarh】^{【103†[9]】} or 【104†Odantapuri】 and was established before 【105†Oxford】, and 【106†Cambridge】 universities or even the 【107†Jagaddala Vihara】.^{【108†[10]】}^{【109†[11]】}^{【110†[12]】} It was made by king Srichandra. Its exact location has not been found.
L172: Chandrapur university was unique in contrast with other universities and the viharas established by Buddhist kings in India. Its curriculum included almost all of the subjects from the Hindu Shastra except the grammar of Chandragomin who was Buddhist by religion. Moreover, the students of this university were also Hindu Brahmins.
L173: The nine monasteries were overseen mainly by Hindu Brahmins.^{【103†[9]】} This is because the Buddhist king, Srichandra, granted lands in Paschimbhag in the name of 【111†Buddha】 for nine Brahmin monasteries where the Chaturvedas were mainly taught. Kamalakanta Gupta referred to this grant as "unique among other Buddhist kings" since it implies Srichandra's favouritism towards Brahmins.
L174: Land distribution among 25 classes of people, guests, and students indicates an efficient administration in the universities. According to Zafir Setu, this administrative arrangement is more consistent with the present-day university system.^{【109†[11]】}
