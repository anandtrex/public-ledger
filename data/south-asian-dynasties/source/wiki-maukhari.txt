L157: The Maukhari dynasty (【103†Gupta script】: , Mau-kha-ri) was a post-【104†Gupta】 dynasty who controlled the vast plains of 【105†Ganga】-【106†Yamuna】 for almost a century from 【64†Kannauj】. They earlier served as vassals of the Guptas and later of 【107†Harsha】's 【108†Vardhana dynasty】. The Maukharis established their independence during the mid-6th century. The dynasty ruled over much of 【109†Uttar Pradesh】 and 【110†Magadha】.
L158: Around 606 CE, a large area of their empire was reconquered by the 【102†Later Guptas】.^{【111†[3]】} According to 【112†Xuanzang】, the territory may have been lost to King 【113†Shashanka】 of the 【75†Gauda Kingdom】, who declared independence circa 600CE.^{【114†[4]】}^{【115†[5]】}
L159: ## Origin
L160: 
L161: [【116†edit】]
L162: Several Maukhari branches were found to be distributed across multiple regions of the subcontinent.^{【117†[6]】} The dynastic name is believed to derive from Mukhara, an eponymous progenitor. According to the 【118†Harshacharita】 by 【119†Bāṇabhaṭṭa】, the marriage between princess Rājyaśrī and 【120†Grahavarman】 united the 【90†Pushyabhuti】 and Mukhara lineages.
L163: The dynasty claimed descent from Ashvapati, who is described in the 【121†Mahabharata】 as the ruler of 【122†Madra】 in the 【123†Punjab】, thereby associating themselves with the Solar dynasty.^{【124†[7]】}
L164: Epigraphic evidence indicates that the Maukhari presence extended beyond northern India. The 【125†Chandravalli】 inscription records that a branch of the family resided in the 【126†Karnataka】 region, in the vicinity of Punnata. Based on references in the 【96†Sanskrit】 play 【127†Kaumudi Mahotsava】, historian E. A. Pires hypothesized that the Maukharis ruled Magadha prior to the emergence of the Gupta Empire.
L165: However, modern historical consensus rejects this theory, as the literary evidence is considered insufficient to support the claim.^{【128†[8]】}
L166: The earliest Maukhari inscription has been found in 【129†Gaya】 dating back to the third-century BCE on a clay seal and the Maukharis continued to have a recorded presence in Gaya early in the 6th century CE.^{【124†[7]】}^{【130†[9]】}
L167: ## History
L168: 
L169: [【131†edit】]
L170: 
L171: The main sources of history regarding the Maukharis of Kannauj include references in texts like the 【118†Harshacharita】 and 【132†Mañjuśrī-mūla-kalpa】, the travelogues of foreign visitors particularly from 【133†China】 and the inscriptions left both by themselves and by their rivals including the 【89†Later Gupta dynasty】.^{【134†[10]】}
L172: After travelling from 【129†Gaya】 in 【135†Bihar】, 【136†Harivarman】, the first Maukhari ruler of this branch left an inscription dated to 487 CE in 【137†Sidhi district】 of 【138†Madhya Pradesh】 and this indicates that he was originally a chief under 【139†Buddhagupta】 of the 【104†Imperial Guptas】. His son, Adityavarman, married the sister of Harshagupta of the Later Gupta dynasty. However the first ruler of this line to use the title of maharajadhiraja was his son, Ishanavarman.
L173: The 【140†Aphsad inscription of Ādityasena】 of the Later Guptas gives information that Ishanavarman defeated the 【79†Alchon Huns】 in battle while the Haraha inscription gives information on further military victories against the 【141†Gaudas】, 【142†Andhras】 and the Sulikas (theorised by some scholars to be the 【143†Chaulukyas】).^{【134†[10]】} Despite marital alliances between them, the Maukharis had a rivalry with the Later Guptas of Magadha.
L174: Ishanavarman was succeeded by one of his sons, Sarvavarman in approximately 560 CE who in turn was succeeded by Avantivarman in the 580s. Avantivarman was known by various titles including 【144†Paramesvara】 and "pride of the race". Under his rule, the Maukhari kingdom spread from 【145†Thaneshwar】 in the West to 【146†Nalanda district】 in the east.^{【134†[10]】}
L175: The last Maukhari ruler was Grahavarman who reigned from c. 600 to 605 CE. He was married to a princess of the 【90†Pushyabhuti dynasty】 leading to an alliance between the two kingdoms however he was killed in battle by 【147†Devagupta】 of 【148†Malwa】 and the Maukhari territories where eventually absorbed into the territory of 【107†Harsha】.^{【134†[10]】}
L176: ### Succession
L177: 
L178: [【149†edit】]
L179: The 【108†Vardhana dynasty】 (also called "Pushyabhuti dynasty") ultimately succeeded the Maukhari dynasty, but it had originally only been a small polity around their capital Sthaneshvara (【150†Thanesar】). According to 【151†Hans T. Bakker】, their ruler Aditya-Vardhana (or Aditya-Sena) was probably a feudatory to the Maukhari ruler 【152†Sharvavarman】. His successor 【153†Prabhakaravardhana】 may have also been a feudatory to the Maukhari king Avantivarman in his early days.
L180: Prabhakara's daughter Rajyashri married Avantivarman's son Grahavarman. As a result of this marriage, Prabhakara's political status increased significantly, and he assumed the imperial title Parama-bhattaraka Maharajadhiraja ("the one to whom the other kings bow because of his valour and affection").^{【154†[11]】}
L181: ## Religion
L182: 
L183: [【155†edit】]
L184: 
L185: The Maukharis were orthodox Hindus. 【97†Hinduism】 received state support, but 【98†Buddhism】 also persisted as a prominent religion.^{【156†[12]】}
L186: ## Army
L187: 
L188: [【157†edit】]
L189: The Maukhari army consisted of elephants, cavalry and infantry. Ishanavarman in all probability would have taken much pains to reorganise the army and make it strong and worthy. The Maukhari strategy mainly focused on deploying elephant corps to crush the enemy armies.
L190: They were used against the 【79†Alchon Huns】 and the 【89†Later Gupta】 armies.^{【156†[12]】} The Maukharis fought against the remnants of the Alchon Huns in the areas of the 【158†Gangetic Doab】 and 【159†Magadha】, as documented in the Aphsad inscription, while the 【160†Aulikaras】 repelled them in the 【148†Malwa】 region.^{【161†[13]】} The Aphsad inscription of 【162†Ādityasena】 mentions the military successes of kings of the 【89†Later Gupta dynasty】 against the Maukharis, themselves past victors of the Alchon Huns:^{【161†[13]】}
L191: > The 【140†Aphsad inscription of Ādityasena】
L192: >
L193: > "The son of that king (【163†Kumaragupta】) was the illustrious 【164†Dâmôdaragupta】, by whom (his) enemies were slain, just like the demons by (the god) 【165†Dâmôdara】. Breaking up the proudly stepping array of mighty elephants, belonging to the Maukhari, which had thrown aloft in battle the troops of the 【79†Hûnas】 (in order to trample them to death), he became unconscious (and expired in the fight)."
L194: — Line 8 of the 【140†Aphsad inscription of Ādityasena】.^{【166†[14]】}
L195: 
L196: The Maukharis, rather than the 【104†Guptas】, were therefore the key actors in repelling the Hunas.^{【167†[15]】}
L197: ## Administration
L198: 
L199: [【168†edit】]
L200: 
L201: 【64†Kanyakubja】, the Maukhari capital, grew in prosperity and importance as a great cosmopolitan city. After the demise of the Maukharis, it even became the capital of Emperor 【107†Harsha】. Hence, Kanyakubja was largely contested by imperial powers.^{【169†[16]】}^{: 20 }^{【156†[12]】}^{【170†[17]】}
L202: The first three Maukhari kings are mentioned in the inscriptions as 【171†Maharaja】, but their successors assumed grander titles showing an increase in power and prestige. Ishanavarman was the first Maukhari ruler to adopt the title Maharajadhiraja (lit., King of Great Kings).^{【156†[12]】}
L203: ## Cultural and international exchanges
L204: 
L205: [【172†edit】]
L206: 
L207: Asirgarh seal inscription of 【152†Sharvavarman】, Maukhari dynasty, 6th century .^{【173†[18]】}^{【174†[19]】}
L208: The Maukhari kings were patrons of poets and writers and many literary works were composed during their reign.^{【156†[12]】} Various seals and inscriptions are known, such as the Asirgarh seal inscription of 【152†Sharvavarman】,^{【175†[20]】} or the 【176†Haraha inscription】 of 【63†Isanavarman】, discovered near the village of Harara in the 【177†Barabanki district】, 【109†Uttar Pradesh】 and dated to 【178†Vikrama Samvat】 610 (ie 554 CE), which record the genealogy of the Maukharis.^{【179†[21]】}
L209: ### Contacts with the Sasanian Empire
L210: 
L211: [【180†edit】]
L212: 
L213: 【92†Sasanian Empire】 King 【181†Khosrow I】 sits before the chessboard, while his vizir and the Indian envoy of 【64†Kannauj】 are playing chess. 【182†Shahnama】, 10th century CE.^{【183†[22]】} The Harahara inscription of 【63†Ishanavarman】. The inscription, dated to 【178†Vikrama Samvat】 610 (ie 554 CE), records the genealogy of the Maukharis.^{【179†[21]】}
L214: With the end of Hunnic power, new contacts were established between India and the 【92†Sasanian Persia】. Intellectual games such as 【184†chess】 and 【185†backgammon】 demonstrated and celebrated the diplomatic relationship between 【181†Khosrow I】 and a "great king of India." The 【186†vizier】 of the Indian king invented chess as a cheerful, playful challenge to Emperor Khosrow.
L215: It seems that the Indian ruler who sent the game of chess to Khosrow was the Maukhari monarch 【187†Śarvavarman】 of 【64†Kannauj】, between the beginning of Śarvavarman's reign in 560/565 and the end of Khosrow's reign in 579.^{【183†[22]】}^{【188†[23]】} When the game was sent to Iran it came with a letter which read: "As your name is the King of Kings, all your emperorship over us connotes that your wise men should be wiser than ours.
L216: Either you send us an explanation of this game of chess or send revenue and tribute us."^{【189†[24]】} Khosrow's grand vizier successfully solved the riddle and figured out how to play chess. In response the wise vizier created the game backgammon and sent it to the Indian court with the same message. The Indian king was not able to solve the riddle and was forced to pay tribute.^{【189†[24]】}

L232: ## Barabar branch of Maukhari rulers
L233: 
L234: [【195†edit】]
L235: 
L236: The 【196†Gopika Cave Inscription】 of Anantavarman (left half).
L237: 
L238: The 【197†Barabar Cave】 inscriptions attest the existence of another Maukhari branch. This branch ruled as feudatories, probably that of the 【102†Later Guptas】. The known rulers of this branch include:^{【198†[28]】}
L239: 
L240:   * Nrpa Shri Yajnavarman
L241:   * Nrpa Samantachudamani Shri Shardulavarman
L242:   * Anantavarman
L243: 
L244:   * 【199†Lomas Rishi】 cave inscription of Anantavarman
L245:   * 【196†Gopika Cave Inscription】 of Anantavarman
L246: 
L247:   * 【200†Vadathika Cave Inscription】 of Anantavarman
L248: In some of the Barabar Caves inscriptions, the words referring to the Ajivikas appear to have been defaced, although the rest of the text appears intact. 【201†E. Hultzsch】 theorised that the defacement took place when the Maukhari ruler Ananta-varman installed Hindu images at the Caves.
L249: However, Basham dismisses this theory, noting that there is little evidence to support this view and the only cave in which the word Ajivikehi remains intact is among the three caves where Ananta-varman installed a Hindu image. According to Basham, since the defacement is selective, it must have taken place when the Brahmi script had still not been forgotten, likely some time before the 5th century CE.^{【202†[29]】}
