L152: 
L153: The Chahamanas of Shakambhari (【81†IAST】: Cāhamāna), colloquially known as the Chauhans of Sambhar or Chauhans of Ajmer, were an Indian dynasty that ruled parts of present-day 【80†Rajasthan】 and neighbouring areas between the sixth and twelfth centuries in the 【82†Indian subcontinent】. The territory ruled by them was known as Sapadalaksha. They were the most prominent ruling family of the 【83†Chauhan clan】 of 【84†Rajputs】.^{【85†[3]】}
L154: The Chahamanas of Shakambhari were initially vassals of the 【86†Gurjara-Pratihara Empire】 until the 10th century, with their original capital at 【87†Shakambhari】 (modern-day 【88†Sambhar】). Following the decline of 【75†Pratihara】 power, the Chahamana ruler 【89†Simharaja】 asserted independence and assumed the title 【69†Maharajadhiraja】.
L155: In the early 12th century, Chahamana power began to rise, and 【90†Ajayaraja II】 transferred the kingdom's capital to 【91†Ajayameru】 (modern-day 【66†Ajmer】), after which the dynasty is also known as the 【92†Chahamanas of Ajmer】, or the 【83†Chauhans】 of Ajmer.
L156: The Chahamanas fought several wars with neighbouring dynasties, including the 【93†Chaulukyas of Gujarat】, the 【76†Tomaras of Delhi】, the 【94†Paramaras of Malwa】, and the 【95†Chandelas of Jejakabhukti】. From the 11th century onward, they also faced invasions from 【96†Muslim】 powers, initially the 【97†Ghaznavids】 and later the 【64†Ghurids】. The kingdom reached its greatest extent under 【58†Vigraharaja IV】 in the mid-12th century.
L157: Chahamana power effectively ended in 1192 CE, when 【98†Muhammad of Ghor】 defeated 【99†Prithviraja III (Prithviraj Chauhan)】 at the 【73†Second Battle of Tarain】 and subsequently executed him, installing his son 【100†Govindaraja IV】 as a 【101†Ghurid】 vassal. Later, 【72†Hariraja】 rebelled against the Ghurids but was defeated in 1194 CE.
L158: Following his defeat, Hariraja, along with members of his family (dynasty), committed suicide by 【102†self-immolation】, bringing the Chahamana dynasty of Shakambhari to an end.
L159: ## Origin
L160: 
L161: [【103†edit】]
L162: According to the 1170 CE 【104†Bijolia】 rock inscription of 【105†Someshvara】,^{【106†[4]】} the early Chahamana king 【107†Samantaraja】 was born at Ahichchhatrapura in the 【108†gotra】 of 【109†sage】 Vatsa.^{【110†[5]】} Historian R. B. Singh theorizes that the Chahamanas probably started out as petty rulers of Ahichchhatrapura (identified with 【111†Nagaur】), and moved their capital to Shakambhari (Sambhar) as their kingdom grew. Later, they became the vassals of the 【112†Imperial Pratiharas】.^{【113†[6]】}
L163: Several mythical accounts of the dynasty's origin also exist. The earliest of the dynasty's inscriptions and literary works state that the dynasty's progenitor was a legendary hero named Chahamana.
L164: They variously state that this hero was born from 【114†Indra】's eye, in the lineage of the sage Vatsa, in the 【115†solar dynasty】 and/or during a ritual sacrifice performed by 【116†Brahma】.^{【117†[7]】} A popular medieval account classifies the dynasty among the four 【118†Agnivanshi】 clans, whose ancestors are said to have come out of 【119†sacrificial fire pit】. The earliest sources to mention this legend are the 16th century recensions of 【120†Prithviraj Raso】.
L165: Some 【121†colonial-era】 historians interpreted this myth to suggest a foreign origin of the dynasty, speculating that the foreign warriors were initiated into the Hindu society through a fire ritual.^{【122†[8]】} However, the earliest extant copy of Prithviraj Raso does not mention this legend at all. Instead, it states that the first ruler of the dynasty was 【123†Manikya Rai】, who is said to have been born from Brahma's sacrifice.^{【124†[9]】}
L166: ## Territory
L167: 
L168: [【125†edit】]
L169: 
L170: The core territory of the Chahamanas was located in present-day 【80†Rajasthan】. It was known as Sapadalaksha (【81†IAST】: Sapādalakṣa) or 【126†Jangala-desha】 (【81†IAST】: Jangaladeśa).^{【127†[10]】}^{【128†[11]】}
L171: The term Jangladesha ("rough and arid country") appears to be older, as it mentioned in the 【129†Mahabharata】.^{【130†[12]】} The text does not mention the exact location of the region. The later Sanskrit texts, such as Bhava Prakasha and Shabdakalpadruma Kosha suggest that it was a hot, arid region, where trees requiring little water grew. The region is identified with the area around 【131†Bikaner】.^{【132†[13]】}
L172: 
L173: 【58†Visaladeva】 inscription on the 【133†Delhi-Topra pillar】, 12th century.
L174: The term Sapadalaksha (literally "one and a quarter 【134†lakhs】" or 125,000) refers to the large number of villages in the area.^{【135†[14]】} It became prominent during the Chahamana reign. It appears that the term originally referred to the area around modern 【111†Nagaur】 near Bikaner. This area was known as Savalak (vernacular form of Sapadalaksha) in as late as 20th century.^{【130†[12]】} The early Chahamana king 【107†Samantaraja】 was based in Ahichchhatrapura, which can be identified with modern Nagaur.
L175: The ancient name of Nagaur was Nagapura, which means "the city of the serpent". Ahichchhatrapura has a similar meaning: "the city whose chhatra or protector is serpent".^{【136†[15]】}
L176: As the Chahamana territory expanded, the entire region ruled by them came to be known as Sapadalaksha.^{【130†[12]】} This included the later Chahamana capitals Ajayameru (【66†Ajmer】) and Shakambhari (【88†Sambhar】).^{【137†[16]】} The term also came to be applied to the larger area captured by the Chahamanas.
L177: The early medieval Indian inscriptions and the writings of the contemporary Muslim historians suggest that the following cities were also included in Sapadalaksha: 【138†Hansi】 (now in 【139†Haryana】), 【140†Mandore】 (now in 【141†Marwar】 region), and 【142†Mandalgarh】 (now in 【143†Mewar】 region).^{【144†[17]】}
L178: ## History
L179: 
L180: [【145†edit】]
L181: 
L182: The 【146†Harshnath】 temple was commissioned by the Chahamana rulers
L183: 
L184: 【147†Bisaldeo temple】 commissioned by 【58†Vigraharaja IV】
L185: 
L186: 【148†Bijolia Parshvanath temple】 built during the reign of King 【105†Somesvara】
L187: The earliest historical Chahamana king is the 6th century ruler 【71†Vasudeva】. According to a legendary account in 【149†Prithviraja Vijaya】, he received the 【150†Sambhar Salt Lake】 as a gift from a 【151†vidyadhara】 (a supernatural being).^{【152†[18]】} Little is known about his immediate successors. The 8th century Chahamana ruler 【153†Durlabharaja I】 and his successors are known to have served the 【154†Gurjara-Pratiharas】 as vassals.
L188: In 10th century, 【155†Vakpatiraja I】 made an attempt to overthrow the Gurjara-Pratihara suzerainty, and assumed the title 【156†Maharaja】 ("great king").^{【157†[19]】} His younger son 【158†Lakshmana】 established the 【159†Naddula Chahamana branch】. Vakpatiraja's elder son and successor 【89†Simharaja】 assumed the title 【69†Maharajadhiraja】 ("king of great kings"), which suggests that he was a sovereign ruler.^{【160†[20]】}
L189: Simharaja's successors consolidated the Chahamana power by engaging in wars with their neighbours, including the 【161†Chaulukyas】 of Gujarat and the 【76†Tomaras】 of Delhi. The dynasty's earliest extant inscription (973 CE) is from the reign of 【162†Vigraharaja II】.^{【135†[14]】} During the reign of 【163†Viryarama】 (r. c.
L190: (circa) 1040 CE), the 【94†Paramara】 king 【164†Bhoja】 invaded the Chahamana kingdom, and probably occupied their capital 【88†Shakambhari】 for a brief period.^{【165†[21]】} 【166†Chamundaraja】 restored the Chahamana power, possibly with the help of the Naddula Chahamanas.^{【165†[21]】}
L191: The subsequent Chahamana kings faced several 【167†Ghaznavid】 raids. 【90†Ajayaraja II】 (r. c. (circa) 1110–1135 CE) repulsed a Ghaznavid attack, and also defeated the Paramara king 【168†Naravarman】.
L192: He moved the kingdom's capital from Shakambhari to Ajayameru (【66†Ajmer】), a city that he either established or greatly expanded.^{【169†[22]】}^{【170†[23]】} His successor 【171†Arnoraja】 raided the Tomara territory, and also defeated the 【97†Ghaznavid】 ruler 【172†Bahram Shah】 in the Slaughter of Turushkas near Ajmer.^{【173†[24]】} However, he suffered setbacks against the Gujarat Chaulukya kings 【174†Jayasimha Siddharaja】 and 【175†Kumarapala】, and was killed by his own son 【176†Jagaddeva】.^{【177†[25]】}
L193: Arnoraja's younger son 【58†Vigraharaja IV】 greatly expanded the Chahamana territories, and captured 【178†Delhi】 from the 【76†Tomaras】. He also defeated 【97†Ghaznavid】 King 【179†Khusrau Shah】 in Vigraharaja IV's first war against the Muslims.^{【180†[26]】} His kingdom included parts of the present-day Rajasthan, Haryana, and Delhi.
L194: It probably also included a part of 【181†Punjab】 (to the south-east of 【182†Sutlej river】) and a portion of the northern 【183†Gangetic plain】 (to the west of 【184†Yamuna】).^{【185†[27]】} His 1164 CE Delhi-Shivalik pillar inscription claims that he conquered the region between the 【186†Himalayas】 and the 【187†Vindhyas】, and thus restored the rule of 【188†Aryans】 in 【189†Aryavarta】. While this is an exaggeration, it is not completely baseless.
L195: The inscription was originally found in Topra village, near the 【190†Shivalik Hills】 (Himalayan foothills). Also, the exiled ruler of 【191†Malwa】 (Vindhyan region) possibly acknowledged his suzerainty. Thus Vigraharaja's influence extended from the Himalayas to the Vindhyas, at least in name.^{【192†[28]】}
L196: The last stand of the Rajputs, depicting the 【73†Second Battle of Tarain】 in 1192, against the forces of the 【193†Ghurid Empire】.
L197: 
L198: Vigraharaja was succeeded by his son 【194†Amaragangeya】, and then his nephew 【195†Prithviraja II】. Subsequently, his younger brother 【105†Someshvara】 ascended the throne.^{【196†[29]】}
L199: The most celebrated ruler of the dynasty was Someshvara's son Prithviraja III, better known as 【197†Prithviraj Chauhan】. He defeated several neighbouring kings, including the 【198†Chandela】 ruler 【199†Paramardi】 in 1182–83, although he could not annex the Chandela territory to his kingdom.^{【200†[30]】} In 1191, he defeated the 【193†Ghurid Empire】 king 【98†Muhammad of Ghor】 at the 【201†first Battle of Tarain】.
L200: However, the next year, he was defeated at the 【73†second Battle of Tarain】 by Muhammad of Ghor, and subsequently killed.^{【202†[31]】}
L201: Muhammad of Ghor appointed Prithviraja's son 【100†Govindaraja IV】 as a vassal. Prithviraja's brother 【72†Hariraja】 dethroned him, and regained control of a part of his ancestral kingdom. Hariraja was defeated by the Ghurids in 1194 CE. Govindaraja was granted the fief of 【203†Ranthambore】 by the Ghurids. There, he established a 【78†new branch】 of the dynasty.^{【204†[32]】}
L202: ## Cultural activities
L203: 
L204: [【205†edit】]
L205: 
L206: Statue of 【206†Neminatha】 (Jainism), Chahamanas Dynasty, 【207†Narhar】, 【80†Rajasthan】, 11th century CE. 【208†National Museum, New Delhi】.
L207: 
L208: The Chahamanas commissioned a number of Hindu temples, several of which were destroyed by the 【101†Ghurid】 invaders after the defeat of 【99†Prithviraja III】.^{【209†[33]】}
L209: Multiple Chahamana rulers contributed to the construction of the 【146†Harshanatha】 temple, which was probably commissioned by 【210†Govindaraja I】.^{【211†[34]】} According to 【149†Prithviraja Vijaya】:
L210:   * 【89†Simharaja】 commissioned a large Shiva temple at 【212†Pushkar】^{【213†[35]】}
L211:   * 【166†Chamundaraja】 commissioned a 【214†Vishnu】 temple at Narapura (modern Narwar in 【215†Ajmer district】)^{【216†[36]】}
L212:   * 【217†Prithviraja I】 built a food distribution centre (anna-satra) on the road to 【218†Somnath temple】 for pilgrims.^{【219†[37]】}
L213:   * 【105†Someshvara】 commissioned a number of temples, including five temples in Ajmer.^{【220†[38]】}^{【221†[39]】}
L214: 【58†Vigraharaja IV】 was known for his patronage to arts and literature, and himself composed the play 【222†Harikeli Nataka】. The structure that was later converted into the 【223†Adhai Din Ka Jhonpra】 mosque was constructed during his reign.^{【224†[40]】}
L215: The Chahamana rulers also patronized 【68†Jainism】.
L216: Vijayasimha Suri's Upadeśāmālavritti (1134 CE) and Chandra Suri's Munisuvrata-Charita (1136 CE) state that Prithviraja I donated golden 【225†kalashas】 (cupolas) for the Jain temples at Ranthambore.^{【226†[41]】} The 【227†Kharatara-Gachchha】-【228†Pattavali】 states that 【90†Ajayaraja II】 allowed the Jains to build their temples in his capital Ajayameru (【66†Ajmer】), and also donated a golden kalasha to a 【229†Parshvanatha】 temple.^{【230†[42]】} Someshvara granted the Revna village to a Parshvanatha temple.^{【220†[38]】} 【231†Bijolia Parshvanath temples】 was constructed in 1160 CE by Mahajan Lala during the reign of King 【105†Somesvara】.
L217: Victory scene, Chahamanas Dynasty, 【232†Sikar】, 【80†Rajasthan】, 10th century CE. 【208†National Museum, New Delhi】.
